## Format 1: Transformations

Transformations are the highest-performing format on Seedance right now. The key is writing each shot individually — number them, describe the exact action in each one, and give Seedance a clear escalation arc: calm → threat → transformation → aftermath.

## Format 2: Orbs

Orbs are single continuous POV shots — the camera is the character's eyes. The formula is always the same: one shot, first-person perspective, hyper-chaotic handheld motion, 15 seconds. What changes is the power, the environment, and the enemy.

> **Pro tip:** Specify the VFX inline inside the action description using brackets — `[VFX: branching electric circuits pulsing with white-blue current]`. This tells Seedance exactly what the power looks like on the character's body without breaking the shot description.

---

## Format 3: POVs

POV is all about locking the perspective and never breaking it. The prompt structure is simple — but the one instruction most people miss is telling Seedance what the camera is **not** doing.

> **Pro tip:** Be explicit about what the camera is NOT doing. "No cuts, no zoom, natural head movement" — that instruction alone is what keeps the perspective locked. Without it Seedance will default to cutting between angles and the whole illusion breaks.

## Format 4: Fights

Fight scenes need three things: a clear location, a clear power mismatch, and a defined escalation arc. The best fight prompts describe the exact choreography beat by beat — Seedance executes what you write.

## Format 5: Animation

Animation prompts in Seedance work best when you break the 15 seconds into timed segments and describe each one explicitly. Use a keyframe image as the style reference, specify the animation aesthetic in the opening line, and describe the physics — particle simulation, dust, energy VFX — as precisely as you describe the character actions.

---
