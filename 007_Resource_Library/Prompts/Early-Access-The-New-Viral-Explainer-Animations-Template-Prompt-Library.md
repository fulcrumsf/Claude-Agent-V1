You may have noticed that stickman-style animations have absolutely exploded on YouTube over the last while.

What surprised me most was that my tutorial on how to make them ended up becoming one of the fastest-growing videos on my channel. It wasn’t something I expected at all. I only made it because people kept asking for it, and honestly, I thought it was almost *too* simple to be interesting.

**But that’s kind of the point.**

Simple formats that are easy to execute, repeat, and adapt tend to spread fast. And once I saw that pattern clearly, I started paying closer attention to what else was working.

Recently, I’ve noticed a **new wave of explainer-style animations** going viral, many of them hitting **hundreds of thousands of views** in a matter of days (I've linked a few examples below👇). They share the same core principles as stickman animations, but with a bit more flexibility in how ideas are presented.

This post is a follow-up to that observation.

### Here’s the high-level, step-by-step approach I’m using....

1. I start by generating a simple, abstract character in ChatGPT using the latest image model. This is important because the newer model is extremely good at maintaining **character consistency**, especially when the character is intentionally minimal.
2. Once I have the scenes generated to match my script, I bring them into Canva and edit them in a very similar way to the original stickman animations. If you’re already familiar with that workflow, this will feel natural.
3. This is optional but very effective. For certain sequences, like the final desk scene, I use [Kling AI](http://klingaiaffiliate.pxf.io/LillyTT) 👈(affiliate link). Here you can simply upload two scenes and let Kling animate them together.

![](https://c10.patreonusercontent.com/4/patreon-media/p/post/148371488/da6fc4cb0aad4d17938310f65fd54e87/eyJhIjoxLCJ3Ijo4MjB9/1.gif?token-hash=xXJU1QaCDniBCIgyhNrwN1TewBYnj7r0gGMJ8IGxueY%3D&token-time=1779580800)

From there, it’s just subtle background music and light sound design. Nothing heavy, just enough to support the pacing.

From concept to finished video, this took about a day all in. Now that the format is locked in, I could realistically produce multiple versions of this per day if I wanted to focus on this niche while it’s hot. "Why don't I?" you may ask, this is a "hot niche", the flood gates will open soon and it will become super-saturated BUT like anything, if you get in early you may be able to establish a brand around it...I already have a brand 😉.
