# Blocking Plan

**Device type: handheld/reactive** — a bystander filming the move on their
phone, not a fixed mounted camera. No fixed geometry to plan; this is a
trigger -> reaction chain instead.

1. Two movers carry a red couch out of a UK terraced house toward an open
   moving-truck ramp parked at the curb. A bystander (neighbor/passerby) is
   loosely filming the move on their phone, dusk light, 2011-era phone
   camera quality.
2. A red fox trots into frame from the side, crossing directly through the
   open ramp threshold near the movers' feet as they're mid-carry.
3. Trigger -> reaction: the bystander's phone whips toward the fox's motion
   — a real untrained person's reflexive camera jerk, not a planned pan.
   Framing loosens/shakes on the whip.
4. The camera partially re-settles as the fox continues past and off-frame;
   movers glance down/back at it mid-carry, couch never set down.

This is the plan the storyboard below must match — subject count (2 movers +
1 fox + couch) stays fixed, the ramp stays open and in the same position
across frames, and the couch's position advances but never resets.

**Mover body orientation lock (added after v4 review) — Mover 1 walks
backward the entire time.** Mover 1 is the front carrier, closer to the
truck/ramp, and walks backward facing Mover 2 for the full duration of the
shot — this is the natural real-world way two people carry a couch (the
lead person walks backward so both can see each other and coordinate). This
orientation is fixed from the very first frame and never changes — Mover 1
never flips to a forward-facing "carrying from behind" grip partway through.
Real defect this fixes: v4's storyboard showed Mover 1 correctly
backward-facing in frame 1, then switched to forward-facing mid-sequence —
an orientation change that can't happen without stopping and physically
repositioning, which doesn't happen in one continuous candid shot.

# Overhead Site Plan (locked geography, text form)

Real cause of frame-2's architecture drift and the couch appearing lifted
over a column: no fixed layout was defined, so the model reinvented the
house/path each frame. Locking it once here:

- Terraced house front door, centered on the property, opening directly onto
  a short straight front path (no turns).
- A low stone/brick retaining wall with a single square gate post runs along
  the property's street-facing edge, with a gap at the path — the path exits
  through that gap, it does not require lifting anything over the post.
- The front path runs straight from the door, through the wall gap, to the
  curb — roughly 15 meters total.
- The moving truck is parked at the curb directly in line with the path, ramp
  extended and touching the pavement at the path's end — the ramp is the
  path's natural continuation, not an separate obstacle.
- Parked cars line the curb on both sides of the truck, not blocking the path
  or the ramp.
- This exact layout is what every environment-sheet panel below must show,
  each from the camera angle that frame needs — same door, same wall/gate
  position, same path line, same ramp alignment, every time.
