# Site Plan — Central Kenya Trading-Center Shopfront (text form, before any image)

**Real route, described as a person/animal would actually travel it:**
the courier rides in from one side, stops his bicycle at the apron, walks
to the counter, sets the clipboard down, hands off the parcel. The monkey
originates from a roof edge/awning post above and to one side, drops down
to the counter, grabs the clipboard, and retreats the same way it came —
a single continuous, physically walkable/climbable path each.

## Coordinate Spec (x = across the shopfront, y = depth from the camera)

| Element | x (across frame) | y (depth) | Extent/notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shop counter/doorway | Center | Mid-depth | Main focal point, goods visible at the edge |
| Awning line | Full width | Upper frame, closer to camera than the counter | Casts a hard shadow edge across the upper-mid frame |
| Monkey origin point (roof edge/awning post) | One side (left), upper frame | Closest to camera, above the awning line | Where the monkey first appears before dropping to counter height |
| Bicycle stopping point/apron | Opposite side (right) or center-right, foreground | Nearest the camera | Where the courier stops and dismounts |
| Clipboard set-down point | On the counter, center | Mid-depth | The pivotal object position — must be clearly visible and trackable |

## Orientation reference

The courier approaches and faces the counter (toward the camera/shop
interior). The monkey's approach is vertical/diagonal (dropping from the
awning post down to counter height), not lateral like the courier's — a
genuinely different orientation axis, stated explicitly so it isn't
confused with a same-plane approach.

## Plausibility check against this written plan

- **Continuous path:** yes — courier's bicycle-apron-to-counter route and
  the monkey's awning-post-to-counter route are both continuous, no gaps.
- **No blocked path:** nothing sits between the monkey's origin point and
  the clipboard's position on the counter.
- **No architectural/construction-logic risk category applies at the
  complex-structure level** — this is a simple single-story shopfront, not
  multi-level load-bearing geometry; the equivalent risk here is counter
  height/awning-post plausibility as a monkey's real climbing route,
  addressed above.
