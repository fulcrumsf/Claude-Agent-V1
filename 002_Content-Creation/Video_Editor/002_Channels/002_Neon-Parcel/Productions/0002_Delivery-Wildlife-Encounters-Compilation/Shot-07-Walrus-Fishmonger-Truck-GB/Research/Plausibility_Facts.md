# Phase 1 — Self-questioning elaboration

1. **Is a walrus in a Scottish harbor actually plausible?** Yes, and unusually well-documented for exactly this reason: multiple real walruses (nicknamed Thor, Wally, Magnus) have hauled out in Scottish/UK/Irish harbors in recent years, always juveniles/subadults, likely displaced by lost sea ice. This shot's premise (a walrus turning up at a Scottish harbor) is grounded in a real, recurring phenomenon, not invented.
2. **How would it end up INSIDE the truck, specifically?** The van picks up its fish order that morning at the harbor dock, where crates are packed in ice with the rear roll-up door open. A stray walrus already hauled out nearby climbs/slides in among the ice and fish while nobody is looking (it is drawn to the smell and the cool ice), and settles in the dark, cool cargo box. Nobody notices before the door is rolled down and the van drives off to the shop.
3. **What size walrus is honest here, given Tony's "large"?** See Concept_Filters.md correction: a subadult, about 2.0-2.3 m long, several hundred kg — visually enormous next to the fishmonger and inside the van, but able to have physically climbed in and to turn and exit through the rear opening. Real Scottish sightings are consistently this size class, never a full breeding-age male.
4. **What does a subadult walrus look like, concretely?** Grey-brown, wrinkled, sparsely-haired thick skin; a rounded blunt snout with a dense pad of stiff white/pale whisker bristles (vibrissae); small eyes; small tusks just emerging (a few centimeters, not the long curved tusks of a mature adult); a thick rolled neck; large front flippers used to drag/push itself; no visible external ears. Walruses move on land in a rolling, heaving "waddle" using their front flippers and body undulation, occasionally using tusks/snout for leverage.
5. **What does the fishmonger's van look like?** Tony's own description: a small box van similar in scale/proportion to a Japanese kei truck, adapted to a UK-market small refrigerated box van (e.g. a Suzuki Carry/Bedford Rascal-class cab-chassis or a small Transit-based box body) with a roll-up (roller-shutter style) rear door on the cargo box, not barn doors. It also has a hydraulic tail lift under the bed at the rear, currently folded up/stowed and NOT deployed — the walrus and ice spill over this closed, stowed lift gate, not onto a lowered platform.
6. **What is inside the cargo box?** A shallow bed of crushed/cube ice filling the floor, with the fish crates now empty or nearly so — the walrus has eaten essentially the entire delivery, leaving fish heads and skeletal remains scattered in the ice (no blood, per Tony's explicit instruction).
7. **Where does the shop sit, and how does the van arrive?** A single-story stone-built shopfront on a coastal village street, CCTV mounted above/beside the shop door, fixed static frame. The van enters from frame-right, drives forward, then turns right to reverse-angle-park (nose partly toward the camera side or parallel, whichever keeps the rear/roll-door facing the camera) at the curb directly in front of the shop, so the opened rear door and the walrus's exit are square-on to the camera.
8. **Midday overcast Scottish coastal light:** flat, soft, cool-grey daylight, damp-looking stone and pavement (recent rain, not currently raining), muted colors, no hard shadows, gulls plausible in the background (not required in-frame).
9. **The fishmonger himself:** a Scottish tradesman in his 40s-50s, practical work clothing (waterproof bib-front apron/oilskin apron over a jumper, wellington boots or rubber boots, maybe a flat cap), no branding/company name needed on his person; his role is opening the shop's own delivery, not wearing a delivery-company uniform.
10. **The reaction:** he unrolls/lifts the roller shutter door, sees the walrus, visibly startles and steps/jumps back half a pace with a hand instinctively raised, then the walrus burps audibly and waddles/half-jumps down out of the cargo box past him, dragging some ice and fish remains with it over the closed lift gate lip.
11. **Where does the walrus go?** Off toward the harbor/water direction (implied off-frame), consistent with a real animal seeking to return to the sea; it does not interact further with the fishmonger or shop.
