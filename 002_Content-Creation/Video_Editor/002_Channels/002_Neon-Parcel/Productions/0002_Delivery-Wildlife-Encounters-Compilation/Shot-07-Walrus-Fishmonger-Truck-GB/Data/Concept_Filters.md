# Concept filters — Shot 07
Tony dictated this concept directly (not a proposed matrix cell). Recorded per the Remarkable-but-Believable filter and one real grounding correction below.

## Remarkable-but-Believable Concept Filter
- Single visual anomaly: a full-grown-looking walrus waddling out of an ordinary delivery van's back door in a village street.
- Why this camera captures it: a fixed shopfront CCTV records the delivery bay outside the fishmonger's every day.
- Ordinary situation that makes it understandable: a walrus stowing away in an open dockside fish delivery, undetected until the doors open again — real UK/Scottish walrus strays are a documented, if rare, phenomenon (see Research/Plausibility_Facts.md).
- Escalation/consequence: the fishmonger jumps back startled; the walrus burps and waddles off past him, ice and empty fish heads spilling out (no blood, per Tony).
- Ending: walrus exits frame; fishmonger left with an empty ice bed and fish heads. Clean stop, no return trip needed.

## Physical-Action Risk Filter
Actions: drive, park/turn, get out, pull open a roll-up door, jump back, walrus waddle/jump down, burp. No fastening, catching or precise handoffs. Rejected as unnecessary: the fishmonger trying to stop or grab the walrus, any dialogue, a second person.

## Grounding correction (Phase 1/2 finding, see Plausibility_Facts.md)
Tony's own wording used "large walrus." A full adult male Atlantic walrus (up to ~1,200 kg, ~3.1 m) would not physically fit standing/turning inside a small box-van cargo body, and would be far too large and dangerous-looking to read as a comedic surprise rather than alarming. **Correction applied:** the walrus is a real-world-plausible SUBADULT/young Atlantic walrus (~2.0-2.3 m, several hundred kg) — still visually enormous relative to the fishmonger and the truck's interior, still clearly a "large walrus" to a viewer, but sized so it can plausibly have climbed into the cargo box at the dock and turned around to exit. This matches the actual pattern of real UK/Scottish walrus visitors, which are consistently reported as juveniles/subadults, never full-grown breeding males.

**2026-09-22 update:** Tony confirmed the subadult walrus size. "Large" meant large relative to the truck's cargo box, which it now fills.
