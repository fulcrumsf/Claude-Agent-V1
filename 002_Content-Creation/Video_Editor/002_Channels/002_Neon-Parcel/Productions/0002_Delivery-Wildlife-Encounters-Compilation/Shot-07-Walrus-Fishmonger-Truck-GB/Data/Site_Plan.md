Wide-angle security lens (about 90-100° across), so the road is visible well to both sides of the parking space.
- **y = 0-2 m, pavement:** a narrow flagstone pavement in front of the camera building, running the full width of the frame (mostly below the bottom edge of the frame; only its far kerb edge is visible).
- **y = 2-8 m, harbour road:** a two-way quayside road (damp dark tarmac with patches of old granite setts), running left-right across the whole frame. No lane markings needed; the near half (y≈2-5) is the westbound lane (traffic moving screen-right to screen-left, UK left-hand traffic), the far half (y≈5-8) is the eastbound lane.
- **y = 8-13 m, quayside parking row:** a row of nose-in parking spaces on the quay, each about 2.5 m wide and 5 m deep, faint worn white lines. **The van's space is the one centred directly in front of the camera (x≈-1.3 to +1.3).** One small hatchback car is parked two spaces to the LEFT (x≈-6.5); the spaces immediately left and right of the van's space are EMPTY (so the fishmonger and walrus have room). - **Slipway (walrus route to the water):** at the FAR LEFT of the frame (x≈-10 to -13), the quay wall has a gap where a stone slipway slopes down from the road level into the harbour water, running away from the camera. Wet, weedy at the bottom.
- **y > 13.6 m, the harbour:** calm grey harbour water. Two or three small fishing boats moored in the middle distance (y≈25-60 m), one small boat tied against the quay wall to the right (x≈+8).
- **Background (y≈80-300 m):** the curved outer stone harbour wall/breakwater with a small light at its end on the left-centre; to the right, the far side of the bay with a row of whitewashed and grey-stone cottages with slate roofs along the waterfront; low green-brown headland/hills behind, under a flat overcast sky. ## Van path (the main plausibility item)
1. Enters at the RIGHT edge of the frame in the near (westbound) lane, y≈3.5, driving straight toward screen-left, parallel to the camera wall.
2. Keeps going straight until its front is at about x≈+4.
3. Turns RIGHT (away from the camera, toward the water), crossing the far lane in one smooth ~90° arc (a small van's turning circle is about 10 m kerb to kerb, so the arc fits in the 6 m road plus the 5 m-deep space).
4. Drives nose-first into the centre space and stops about 0.5 m short of the quay wall. Engine off; it never moves again.

**Final orientation, by literal visible parts:** the van's FRONT BUMPER, HEADLIGHTS AND WINDSCREEN face the WATER (away from the camera). Its REAR BUMPER, ROLLER-SHUTTER REAR DOOR AND STOWED TAIL LIFT face the CAMERA, square-on, with the rear of the van at about y≈8.3 m (the far edge of the road), roughly 8 m from the camera. The van is parallel to the parking lines, not angled.

## Plausibility check against this written plan (not against an image)
- **Turn geometry:** 6 m of road + a 5 m-deep space gives about 11 m for a ~90° right turn; a compact van needs about 10 m kerb to kerb. Tight but real; this is how quayside nose-in parking is used every day. PASS
- **UK traffic side:** westbound in the near lane, turning right across the far lane into the space. Correct for left-hand traffic. PASS
- **Rear to camera:** nose-in parking in the space directly in front of the camera puts the rear door square-on to the camera, which is exactly what Tony described. PASS
- **Nothing blocks the paths:** the spaces either side of the van are empty; the road behind the van is clear; the walrus's route along the far side of the road to the slipway crosses no kerb or railing (the slipway is a gap in the quay wall at road level). PASS
- **Safety logic:** the quay wall + railing is between the parked van and the drop into the water. PASS
- **Depth order (near to far):** pavement → road → van (rear at ~8 m, front at ~13 m) → quay wall + railing → harbour water + moored boats → breakwater and far-shore cottages → hills. Consistent. PASS
- **Materials:** stone quay wall, stone slipway, flagstone pavement, stone and whitewashed cottages with slate roofs; no brick anywhere. PASS
