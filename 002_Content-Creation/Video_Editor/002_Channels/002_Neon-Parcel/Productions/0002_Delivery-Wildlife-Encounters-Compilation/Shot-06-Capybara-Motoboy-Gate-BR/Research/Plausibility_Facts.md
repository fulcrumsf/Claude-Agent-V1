# Phase 1 — Self-questioning elaboration (questions generated from this shot's own cell)

1. **Where exactly is "a gated condominium in the São Paulo metro suburbs"?** A condomínio fechado (walled residential estate) with a guardhouse (guarita/portaria) and, per the reference sign photo, a separate **service/delivery entrance** ("Entrada para entregas e serviços") distinct from the visitor portaria. Real houses/estates of this type exist across Barueri/Santana de Parnaíba/Cotia-type suburbs. The location is fictional but of this real type.
2. **What is a "motoboy" in 2016 São Paulo, concretely?** A motorcycle courier on a small-displacement street bike (Honda CG 150 Titan/Fan class, 125-160 cc; reference: silver 2009 Titan CG 150 Mix). Gear: helmet (capacete, often open-face jet style), reflective vest or jacket with reflective stripes, jeans, sneakers (tênis) or work boots, gloves, an insulated delivery box (baú) bolted on the rear rack OR an insulated backpack (mochila térmica). In the reference photo the courier has an orange top-box on a small motorcycle.
3. **Which delivery app/brand?** None shown. Unbranded gear: no logos, no readable text. (iFood was the dominant Brazilian app in 2016; Uber Eats only launched in Brazil in Nov 2016, so avoid any specific app look.)
4. **How does a condo delivery actually work in Brazil?** The common rule: "entrega na portaria" — the courier stops at the portaria, the porteiro (doorman) phones the resident, the resident comes down to the gate to collect; the courier usually does not enter. Access cameras require the courier to remove his helmet for identification. So the courier arrives, parks, removes the helmet, and WAITS outside at the guardhouse window (a few minutes). This is the natural setting for a waiting gag; he does not ride in.
5. **What does the guardhouse look like?** Small masonry booth, large tinted safety-glass window (blindex) facing the entry lane, a parcel drawer/slot (passa-volumes) under the window, a projecting concrete awning (marquise) over the front, a fluorescent light under it, a service counter ledge, a buzzer/intercom panel. The awning keeps a slab dry in rain.
6. **What does the ground look like?** A concrete or interlocking-paver slab (calçada/piso intertravado) under the awning; the lane in front is asphalt with a concrete curb (meio-fio); a grass verge with low shrubs and a small stormwater channel along the perimeter wall.
7. **Weather at dusk in a São Paulo summer?** Warm (22-26 °C), humid; brief light drizzle (garoa) is typical in the Dec-Mar season. Surfaces outside the awning are dark and wet and reflect streetlights; the slab under the awning stays dry (lighter tone).
8. **What lights the scene at dusk?** Deep-blue-grey twilight sky; streetlights (in 2016 São Paulo mostly sodium-vapor, warm orange) just switched on; the guardhouse's cool-white fluorescent under the awning; the window glass reflects.
9. **What does a 2016 condo CCTV frame look like?** A 720p-1080p IP or analog-HD camera on a lamp-post/wall bracket across the bay (~3 m high) facing the portaria approach, wide-angle with mild barrel distortion, moderate compression, noise in low light, no pan/tilt/zoom, slightly high mount looking down. No burned-in timestamp is used in generation (text overlays are post-production only).
10. **What does a capybara do here?** A single adult grazing on the grass verge at dusk (crepuscular), unhurried walk, then lies down. **Correction from the earlier idea:** capybaras are semi-aquatic and are NOT bothered by rain, so it is not "sheltering". Its own reason for lying on the slab: flat, level, warm concrete near a calm person. The dry-spot gag lives in the COURIER'S sincere belief ("it took the dry spot"), which is what makes him step out into the drizzle. That keeps the animal behavior honest.
11. **Is a lone capybara plausible?** Capybaras are social, but single adults (often young males) are regularly seen alone at the edges of groups in urban green areas. One adult is fine. Real urban presence: established along the Pinheiros/Tietê river corridors and green belts of the São Paulo metro area.
12. **How big is everything relative to everything?** Capybara: 35-66 kg, up to ~1.3 m long, ~0.5-0.6 m at the shoulder. Courier ~1.70 m. Motorcycle ~2.0 m long, seat ~0.78 m high. Awning clearance ~2.7 m. Window sill ~1.1 m. So the capybara is knee-to-thigh height on the courier, shorter than the bike seat, longer than the courier's stride.
13. **Could the capybara be on the seat?** No (see Concept_Filters.md): seat height exceeds its climbing/jump ability.
14. **How does the courier react, physically?** Sincere and gentle: eyes drop, a small breath out, weight shifts back one step, a half-smile, a slight lift of both open hands ("all yours"), steps sideways out of the dry patch keeping the animal in view, hunches shoulders as drizzle hits, puts the helmet on for the rain. No shouting, no rush, no shooing.
15. **What does the porteiro do?** Off-camera behind tinted glass; never visible (adds no third subject).
