# Blocking Plan

**Device type: handheld/reactive (consumer camcorder)** — same broad
device category as Shot 01 (movers/fox, UK), but era-shifted: mid-2000s
standard-definition camcorder rather than a modern smartphone. Natural
human handheld motion is expected and correct here (unlike Shot 02's fixed
doorbell mount or Shot 03's vehicle-borne dash-cam) — the filmer's own
hand-shake, reframing, and a reactive whip-turn toward the tanuki are all
in-character for this device type, not defects.

1. A resident films from their own rural property at dusk, light rain
   falling, as a red postal delivery scooter (generic livery, boxy rear
   cargo case, no reproduced real postal-service logo — Phase 1,
   `Research/Plausibility_Facts.md`) arrives along the narrow village road
   and stops briefly at the property gate.
2. The postal carrier completes the delivery at the gate (mail/parcel
   handed off or placed in a delivery slot) and the scooter departs back
   down the road, engine running, headlight on given the fading dusk
   light.
3. As the scooter's sound fades, a tanuki (Phase 1: *Nyctereutes
   procyonoides viverrinus*, roughly cat-to-small-dog sized — notably
   smaller than the kangaroo or moose calf in prior shots) ambles out from
   the roadside vegetation/wooded hillside, low and cautious, sniffing at
   the wet ground.
4. Trigger → reaction: the filmer notices the tanuki and the camera
   reacts naturally — a handheld whip/reframe toward it, in-character for
   this device type (contrast with Shot 02/03's fixed mounts, which could
   never do this). The tanuki freezes briefly at the sudden attention,
   ears and nose twitching — a real physical tell (Phase 2), not a static
   pose.
5. The tanuki resumes its low, cautious movement, ambling back toward the
   hillside vegetation and out of frame at its own pace; the camera may
   hold or gently follow, still handheld, still reactive.

This is the plan the storyboard must match — subject count (1 postal
carrier/scooter, 1 tanuki, appearing sequentially not simultaneously
required to interact) stays fixed, and the handheld camera's natural human
motion is itself part of the shot's authenticity, not something to
suppress.

**Real-world scale note (Phase 1/2, `Research/Plausibility_Facts.md`):** a
tanuki is roughly cat-to-small-dog sized (~50-60cm body length, ~20-25cm
shoulder height) — use the scooter's wheel and the property gate as
in-frame scale references. This is a real, deliberate scale contrast with
Shot 02's kangaroo and Shot 03's moose calf, not an inconsistency between
shots — different species, different real sizes.

**Natural motion note (per locked v2 plausibility check, extended to this
shot):** the tanuki's freeze needs a real physical tell (ear/nose
twitching, a brief full-body stillness) before it resumes moving — not a
frozen illustration pose. The camcorder's own reactive whip-turn toward it
should read as a genuine human startle-response, not a smooth cinematic
pan.

**Species/behavior note (Phase 1 elaboration):** tanuki are genuinely
crepuscular/nocturnal — the dusk timing isn't just a matrix-cell
coincidence, it's the real behavioral reason this species would plausibly
appear now. Its build is canid (not procyonid/raccoon) despite the
raccoon-like facial mask — stocky, low-slung, coarse grayish-brown fur.

**Weather note (Diversity Matrix weather axis):** light rain, wet
reflective road surface, fading dusk light, scooter headlight on. Must
stay consistent across every panel/frame of this shot.

**On-screen text note (Phase 1 elaboration — same rule tension as Shot
03, resolved the same way):** some real mid-2000s camcorders burn in a
date/battery-indicator overlay. **Do not render this inside any
generation prompt** — add it afterward via compositing if wanted, never
inside the Seedance/GPT-Image-2 prompt itself.

# Handheld Camcorder Frame (locked geography, text form)

- Standard-definition, mid-2000s consumer camcorder look: 4:3 or early
  transitional 16:9 framing, softer SD-era detail, natural (not
  digitally stabilized) handheld shake, muted/slightly washed color
  rendering typical of that hardware generation (Phase 1).
- Narrow paved rural village road runs through the frame, no reliable
  painted centerline assumed — many rural Japanese roads of this type are
  narrower, sometimes single-lane-with-passing-points.
- The filmer's own property line — a traditional wooden fence or hedge,
  with a gate/mailbox area — borders one side of the road; this is where
  the postal scooter stops.
- A wooded, terraced hillside borders the opposite side of the road —
  this is where the tanuki enters and exits, consistent with the
  mountain-adjacent rural Honshu setting (Phase 1).
- Light rain throughout, wet reflective road surface, fading dusk
  ambient light plus the scooter's own headlight while it's present in
  frame.
- This exact geography — same road, same property gate, same hillside
  treeline — is what every storyboard panel must show; only the
  subjects (scooter, tanuki) and the camera's own natural handheld
  motion change.
