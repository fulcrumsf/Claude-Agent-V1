# Blocking Plan

**Device type: fixed/mounted, static location camera** — same broad
category as Shot 02 (doorbell) and Shot 03's environment logic, but a
shopfront CCTV rather than a doorbell or vehicle mount: fixed non-pan/
tilt, mounted under the shop's awning looking out over the counter and
apron (Phase 1, `Research/Plausibility_Facts.md`).

1. A bicycle courier (generic, unbranded parcel bicycle, no reproduced
   real postal-service logo) rides into frame and stops at the shop
   counter, dismounting to hand off a small parcel.
2. To free both hands for the parcel, he sets his clipboard (with a
   single visible delivery slip clipped to it, pen attached) down on the
   counter edge — this is the pivotal vulnerable moment the whole gag
   depends on.
3. **Comedic hook, the shot's actual payoff:** a vervet monkey, already
   nearby (roof edge/awning post), darts in and grabs the clipboard the
   instant it's set down. It drags the attached pen across the paper in a
   rough scratching motion — reads as "signing" without claiming literal
   comprehension (Phase 1 item 4) — before the courier can react.
4. Trigger → reaction: the courier notices and makes a startled grab/
   lunge toward the monkey, too late. The monkey holds the clipboard just
   out of reach for a beat — a real physical tell (weight shift, tail
   flick, a quick glance at the courier) — not a static frozen pose.
5. The monkey flees with the clipboard back the way it came (roof edge/
   awning post), the courier left empty-handed at the counter. Shot ends
   on the courier's reaction, clipboard gone.

This is the plan the storyboard must match — subject count (1 courier, 1
monkey) stays fixed, and the clipboard itself is a tracked prop that must
stay visually consistent (same light color, same single visible paper
sheet, same attached pen) from the moment it's introduced to the moment it
disappears from frame.

**Real-world scale note (Phase 1/2, `Research/Plausibility_Facts.md`):** a
vervet monkey is roughly 40-60cm body length (plus tail) — cat-to-small-
dog body size excluding the tail, notably smaller than the courier. Use
the counter height and the clipboard itself as in-frame scale references.

**Natural motion note (per locked v2 plausibility check, extended to this
shot):** the "signing" motion must read as a real scratch/drag action with
the pen still attached to the clipboard, not the monkey holding a
detached pen like a person. The grab-and-flee itself needs a real
physical tell (a beat of holding the clipboard just out of reach, a tail
flick) before it fully exits — not an instant teleport out of frame.

**Species/behavior note (Phase 1 elaboration):** vervet monkey
(*Chlorocebus pygerythrus*) — grey-green-tinged fur, black face, long
tail, small dexterous hands. Diurnal and genuinely drawn to novel
handheld objects in trading-center settings — the midday timing and the
clipboard-grab premise are both real behavioral grounding, not invented
for the gag.

**Weather note (Diversity Matrix weather axis):** clear, hot, dry-season
midday sun, hard shadows under the awning line, dry dusty ground — no wet
surfaces, no overcast flatness. Distinct from every prior shot's weather
condition. Must stay consistent across every panel/frame of this shot.

# Shopfront CCTV Frame (locked geography, text form)

- Fixed, non-pan/tilt static CCTV view, mounted under the shop's awning,
  looking out over the counter and the dirt/paved apron in front (Phase
  1) — modest consumer-grade dynamic range, no artificial sharpening.
- The shop counter/doorway occupies the mid-frame, goods/produce visible
  at the counter edge, concrete-block or corrugated-iron shop
  construction.
- The awning line crosses the upper frame, casting a hard shadow edge
  given the clear midday sun.
- The apron in front of the shop — dirt or paved — is where the courier
  stops his bicycle; there is a plausible roof edge or awning post nearby
  where the monkey can plausibly originate from and flee back to.
- This exact fixed frame and layout is what every storyboard panel must
  show — same counter, same awning, same shop front — only the courier,
  the clipboard, and the monkey move within it.
