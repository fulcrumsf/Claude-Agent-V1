# Phase 1 — Self-Questioning Elaboration

Generated fresh from this specific matrix cell, not from a template:

1. **"Rural Kenya" is too vague — which specific kind of place?** A small
   peri-urban trading center in the central Kenya highlands (the general
   character of areas around Nyeri, Murang'a, or similar) — a cluster of
   small shops/dukas along a paved or dirt road, not a major city and not
   uninhabited bush. This is real vervet-monkey territory: vervets are
   genuinely notorious in Kenya for raiding markets and trading centers,
   not just rural farmland — this premise is well-documented, not
   invented.
2. **"Bicycle courier" — what is he actually delivering, and to whom?**
   A local parcel/mail courier delivering a small package to a shop —
   generic unbranded bicycle with a rear rack/basket for parcels, no
   reproduced real postal-service logo (same rule as every prior shot).
   Carries a clipboard with a delivery slip for the shopkeeper to sign —
   this is the actual object the comedic hook depends on, so it must be
   visually distinct and trackable (a light-colored clipboard, a single
   visible paper sheet clipped to it).
3. **Why would a vervet monkey specifically go for a clipboard, not the
   package itself?** Vervets are highly dexterous, visually curious, and
   famously drawn to novel handheld objects (documented real behavior:
   grabbing sunglasses, phones, food wrappers from people in trading
   centers/tourist areas) — a flat, light, easily-gripped clipboard is a
   more plausible grab than a taped/boxed parcel. Conclusion: the monkey
   targets the clipboard specifically because it's the loose, graspable
   object set down within reach, not the sealed package.
4. **What does "mimics the signing gesture" actually look like as real
   describable monkey behavior, not a cartoon?** Vervets are known
   imitators of simple handled-object motions when highly food/object
   motivated (swiping/scratching a stick or object against a surface).
   Concrete depiction: the monkey grips the clipboard and drags/scratches
   the pen (still clipped to it) across the paper in a rough back-and-forth
   motion — reads as "signing" to a viewer without claiming the monkey
   understands writing. This must be described as an emergent-looking
   physical action, not literal comprehension.
5. **What does the shopfront itself look like?** A small single-story
   duka-style shop, painted concrete-block or corrugated-iron construction,
   an open-fronted counter or doorway, a covered awning (common in this
   region, doubles as CCTV mounting point), goods/produce visible under
   the awning, a dirt or paved apron in front where the courier stops his
   bicycle.
6. **"Static shopfront CCTV" — is this actually plausible for a small
   rural Kenyan trading-center shop, era-correct for present day?** Yes —
   small-shop CCTV (often paired with mobile-money agent security) is
   increasingly common in Kenyan trading centers today, mounted under the
   shop awning looking out over the counter/apron area, fixed, non-
   pan/tilt, modest consumer-grade dynamic range.
7. **What is "clear, hot, dry season" doing to the scene?** Bright direct
   midday sun, hard shadows under the awning line, dry dusty ground/apron,
   no wet-surface reflections — must stay consistent, distinct from every
   prior shot's rain/snow/overcast conditions.
8. **Scale/behavior check for the vervet specifically.** Vervet monkeys are
   small — roughly 40-60cm body length (plus a longer tail), notably
   smaller than a person, about house-cat-to-small-dog body size excluding
   tail. Diurnal (day-active) — the midday timing is real behavioral
   grounding, not incidental. Distinct grey-green-tinged fur, black face,
   long tail — must not be rendered as a generic "monkey" without these
   specific field marks.
