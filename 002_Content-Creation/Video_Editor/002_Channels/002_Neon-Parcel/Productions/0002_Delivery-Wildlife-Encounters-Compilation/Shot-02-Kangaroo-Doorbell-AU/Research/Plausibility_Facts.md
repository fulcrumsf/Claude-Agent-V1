# Phase 1 — Self-Questioning Elaboration

Generated fresh from this specific matrix cell, not from a template:

1. **"Food/takeaway delivery" — which specific service is realistic here?**
   A national aggregator (UberEats-style) had mostly metro/inner-city driver
   coverage by 2019; a bushland-fringe regional town is unlikely to have had
   that density of gig drivers yet. Menulog had broader regional Australian
   reach by 2019, but the more realistic default for a small regional town is
   a **local takeaway shop running its own delivery** (pizza, Chinese, or
   fish-and-chips shop with an in-house driver) rather than an app-aggregator
   courier. Conclusion: local takeaway shop's own driver, not a gig-app
   courier.
2. **What is he actually carrying, and how is it packaged?** A local
   takeaway's own driver typically carries a **plain insulated delivery bag
   or a stack of foil/plastic takeaway containers**, not a branded courier
   backpack (that's an aggregator-driver look). No real-world restaurant
   brand/logo — keep it generic.
3. **What does the delivery car look like?** An ordinary older-model sedan
   or hatchback appropriate to a small regional-town takeaway business in
   2019 — not a rideshare-branded vehicle, not a new/luxury model.
4. **What's actually in a rural/bushland-fringe Australian yard?** Realistic
   set dressing for this region: a Hills Hoist rotary clothesline, a second
   vehicle or ute in the driveway, a rainwater tank, native/bushland-style
   garden plantings rather than a manicured lawn. Explicitly deciding **no
   dog** in this shot — a dog would be a plausible yard element but adding
   one introduces an uncontrolled extra subject the Blocking Plan doesn't
   account for; keep subject count at exactly driver + kangaroo.
5. **What does the house/porch itself look like for this region?** A
   single-story brick-veneer or weatherboard project home with a corrugated-
   iron roof — the ordinary regional-Australian house type, visually
   distinct from Test Shot 02's UK terraced-house brick front.
6. **What's the ground/weather condition doing to the scene?** Clear, dry,
   midday sun — hard shadows, no wet pavement or overcast flatness. Must
   stay consistent across every panel/frame of this shot.
7. **"Kangaroo" — which species is actually plausible for a bushland-*fringe*
   regional town, as opposed to true arid outback?** Eastern/western grey
   kangaroo (*Macropus giganteus*/*fuliginosus*) is the species associated
   with bushland-fringe and coastal-regional towns; red kangaroo (*Osphranter
   rufus*) is the true arid-interior/outback species. Conclusion: grey
   kangaroo is the correct species for this setting, not red — confirms the
   character sheet's existing choice, this time with the reasoning shown.
7b. **What exactly gets left at the door — the whole bag, or just the food?**
   Real correction (Tony, 2026-09-18, caught on storyboard review): a
   driver keeps his insulated delivery bag/carrying case — only the food
   item(s)/container(s) taken out of it get left on the doormat. This
   question was never asked during the original Phase 1 pass, so the
   Blocking Plan and storyboard spec had him leaving the entire bag behind,
   which no real driver would do (he needs the bag for the next delivery).
   Corrected in the Blocking Plan and Storyboard Spec.
8. **"Fixed/mounted doorbell camera" — what did a mainstream consumer unit
   actually look like/behave like specifically in 2019?** Ring
   Doorbell 2/Pro-era hardware: wide fisheye lens, motion-triggered clip
   start (not continuously recording), fixed non-pan/tilt mount at door
   height, modest dynamic range/color rendering by later standards. Confirms
   the environment plate's existing framing choice, reasoning shown.
