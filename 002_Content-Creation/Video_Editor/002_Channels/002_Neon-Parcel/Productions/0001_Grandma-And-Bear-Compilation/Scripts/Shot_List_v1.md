# Grandma And Bear Compilation

## Brief

This compilation follows different grandmothers who treat encounters with
wild bears as ordinary household, neighborhood, or customer-service problems.
Every scenario should look like it came from a different real camera and a
different person, while the comedy escalates from calm familiarity to a sharp
reversal or physical payoff.

The visual language is anchored to Reference 1's believable surveillance
footage, while Reference 6 informs the dense multi-moment compilation rhythm.
The long-form video is a standalone compilation with no Part 1/Part 2 label.
Narration is added only after the approved clips are assembled and only where
it adds perspective, setup, reaction, or a useful transition.

## Editorial Rules For This Draft

- Estimated duration is a guide, not a fixed generation length.
- Each clip needs one clear setup, action, and payoff.
- Each clip uses a distinct camera source, location, human character, bear
  behavior, and in-scene voice when dialogue is needed.
- The first two seconds establish the premise but do not reveal the best
  payoff.
- Narration role is tentative until the rough-cut editorial pass.
- These are original scenarios inspired by abstract patterns, not remakes of
  the references.

## Shot List

1. **The porch regular:** A fixed night security camera catches a grandmother
   calmly setting out a plate for a bear, then hiding the plate when it brings
   an empty bowl back and taps it against the door. (12–16s; in-scene voice:
   off-camera grandmother, affectionate and matter-of-fact; narration: none)
2. **The wildlife warning:** A park-ranger body camera records an officer
   warning a grandmother not to feed a bear, and she quietly points out that
   the bear has better table manners than her adult children. (10–14s;
   in-scene voices: off-camera male ranger, on-camera grandmother; narration:
   none)
3. **The grocery delivery:** A doorbell camera shows a bear taking a grocery
   bag from a porch while the grandmother opens the door and complains that it
   substituted the wrong brand of marshmallows. (9–13s; in-scene voice:
   off-camera grandmother through the door; narration: reaction optional)
4. **The garden dispute:** A backyard camera shows a bear flattening a small
   vegetable garden while the grandmother measures the damage, then hands it a
   rake as if assigning a chore. (11–15s; in-scene voice: on-camera
   grandmother, stern but calm; narration: none)
5. **The window customer:** A phone video from inside a kitchen captures a
   bear pressing its nose to the glass while the grandmother holds up a number
   card and tells it to wait its turn. (8–12s; in-scene voice: off-camera
   grandmother, polite customer-service tone; narration: none)
6. **The lawn-chair inspection:** A fixed driveway camera shows a bear testing
   every lawn chair before choosing the grandmother's, and she returns with a
   handwritten reservation sign. (10–14s; in-scene voice: on-camera
   grandmother; narration: setup optional)
7. **The porch swing complaint:** A neighbor's handheld phone footage shows a
   bear occupying a porch swing while the grandmother argues that it is
   rocking too loudly during her afternoon nap. (9–13s; in-scene voice:
   off-camera neighbor and on-camera grandmother; narration: none)
8. **The trash-can misunderstanding:** A side-yard security camera shows a
   grandmother labeling a bear's favorite trash can "reserved" and then
   defending the arrangement to a confused wildlife officer. (12–16s;
   in-scene voices: off-camera officer, on-camera grandmother; narration:
   reaction optional)
9. **The picnic rules:** A family phone video shows a grandmother laying out a
   picnic blanket, pointing to a handwritten rule sheet, and calmly removing a
   bear's paw from the dessert. (11–15s; in-scene voice: on-camera
   grandmother, teacherly tone; narration: none)
10. **The camera-holder's evidence:** A body-cam view follows an officer
    approaching a grandmother and bear, but the grandmother asks the officer
    to document that the bear returned her missing slipper. (10–14s; in-scene
    voices: concerned officer and amused grandmother; narration: none)
11. **The mailbox delivery:** A rural mailbox camera catches a bear blocking
    the mailbox while the grandmother negotiates a delivery exchange: one
    package for one step backward. (9–13s; in-scene voice: off-camera
    grandmother; narration: transition optional)
12. **The barbecue judge:** A patio phone recording shows a bear inspecting a
    grill while the grandmother gives it a disappointed score for seasoning.
    The bear slowly turns away with the spatula. (10–14s; in-scene voice:
    on-camera grandmother; narration: none)
13. **The snow-day neighbor:** A low porch camera shows a grandmother shoveling
    a narrow path for a bear, then closing the path when it refuses to move
    its paws. (11–15s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother; narration:
    reaction optional)
14. **The garden fence meeting:** A static backyard camera shows a bear on one
    side of a fence and a grandmother on the other, each waiting for the other
    to apologize after a small property dispute. (12–16s; in-scene dialogue:
    alternating off-camera voices; narration: none)
15. **The camping check-in:** A handheld flashlight video shows a grandmother
    checking a bear's name against a campsite reservation list and assigning
    it the tent farthest from the coffee. (10–14s; in-scene voice: on-camera
    grandmother; narration: setup optional)
16. **The porch choir:** A doorbell camera records a grandmother humming to a
    bear until it makes one loud growl, after which she stops and gives it a
    conductor's disappointed look. (8–12s; in-scene voice: on-camera
    grandmother; narration: none)
17. **The lost-and-found:** A store parking-lot camera shows a bear carrying a
    child's red wagon toward a grandmother, who checks a lost-and-found tag
    before accepting it. (10–14s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother;
    narration: context optional)
18. **The porch thermostat:** A fixed indoor camera sees a bear outside the
    open door while the grandmother adjusts the thermostat and tells it that
    guests cannot keep leaving the door open. (9–13s; in-scene voice:
    off-camera grandmother; narration: none)
19. **The fishing lesson:** A lakeside phone video shows a grandmother
    correcting a bear's fishing technique, then handing it a tiny folding
    chair when it catches nothing. (11–15s; in-scene voice: on-camera
    grandmother; narration: reaction optional)
20. **The traffic stop:** A dashcam captures a bear crossing in front of a
    slow-moving golf cart driven by a grandmother, who rolls down the window
    and asks it to show its license. (10–14s; in-scene voices: grandmother
    and off-camera passenger; narration: none)
21. **The porch package:** A security camera shows a bear guarding a delivered
    package while the grandmother attempts to retrieve it using increasingly
    polite customer-service language. (12–16s; in-scene voice: off-camera
    grandmother; narration: setup optional)
22. **The bath-time refusal:** A bathroom-side phone recording shows a bear
    refusing to leave a shallow outdoor tub while the grandmother holds up a
    towel and counts down like a parent. (9–13s; in-scene voice: on-camera
    grandmother; narration: none)
23. **The farmer's market cashier:** A wide market security camera shows a
    bear placing one apple on a scale while a grandmother insists it cannot
    leave without paying. (10–14s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother and
    vendor; narration: none)
24. **The porch repair:** A phone camera shows a bear leaning against a loose
    porch rail while a grandmother brings out a toolbox and tells it not to
    touch anything until the contractor arrives. (11–15s; in-scene voice:
    on-camera grandmother; narration: context optional)
25. **The rain delay:** A fixed driveway camera shows a grandmother and bear
    waiting under the same umbrella, both looking toward a closed gate. The
    bear opens the gate first and the grandmother reacts as if it broke a rule.
    (10–14s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother; narration: none)
26. **The birthday guest:** A living-room phone video catches a bear reaching
    toward a birthday cake while the grandmother blocks it with a serving
    plate and redirects it to a smaller treat. (9–13s; in-scene voice:
    on-camera grandmother; narration: reaction optional)
27. **The quiet-hours notice:** A hallway security camera shows a bear making
    noise outside at night while a grandmother places a handwritten quiet-hours
    notice on the glass. (8–12s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother;
    narration: none)
28. **The weather reporter:** A porch phone video shows a grandmother giving a
    serious weather update while a bear slowly appears behind her and changes
    the forecast. (10–14s; in-scene voice: on-camera grandmother; narration:
    none)
29. **The final warning:** A wildlife body camera records a grandmother
    delivering a formal warning to a bear about stealing her casserole, then
    the bear returns with the empty dish clean. (12–16s; in-scene voices:
    off-camera officer and on-camera grandmother; narration: reaction
    optional)
30. **The repeat customer:** A dawn security camera shows the same type of
    encounter from a completely different house and camera angle, but this
    grandmother recognizes the bear's order and calmly adds one extra serving.
    (12–16s; in-scene voice: off-camera grandmother; narration: closing
    transition optional)
