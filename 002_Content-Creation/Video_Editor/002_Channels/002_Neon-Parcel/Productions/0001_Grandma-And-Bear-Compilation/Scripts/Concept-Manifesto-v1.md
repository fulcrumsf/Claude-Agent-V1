# Grandma And Bear Compilation

## Purpose

This manifesto is the source of truth for concepts 6–12 before prompts,
storyboards, or paid video generations are created. It describes the intended
visual logic, not a word-for-word prompt. Each concept must still pass its own
camera-plausibility, physics, visual-realism, and holistic humor review.

These concepts use “grandma” broadly to mean a believable older-woman figure.
The woman must plausibly belong in the environment and must not be inserted
just to repeat a familiar reaction pattern. The humor should come from a rare,
visually clear, plausible absurdity rather than forced dialogue.

## Shared Rules

- Treat each concept as a complete captured event with a clear beginning,
  visible development, and natural endpoint.
- Preserve realistic scale, animal behavior, object quantities, and cause and
  effect.
- Match the camera to who could realistically capture the moment.
- Do not add a return trip, extra prop, dialogue line, or reaction beat unless
  it improves the visual event.
- Use international settings or unusual bear species sparingly; they are
  optional variation, not the organizing formula.
- Do not copy the settings, actions, or camera sources of earlier approved
  concepts. Approved concepts teach overall quality, not reusable ingredients.

## Concept 6 — Driveway Gate

- **Filming device:** Neighbor’s handheld smartphone
- **Framing:** Medium-wide view from the edge of the neighboring driveway;
  slight natural hand movement follows the event without chasing the bear
- **Environment:** Ordinary suburban driveway with an unlocked metal gate,
  parked car, and a visible path beyond it
- **Lighting:** Neutral late-afternoon daylight
- **Tone:** Deadpan, observational
- **Visual flow:** The older woman approaches and opens the driveway gate.
  A bear is already occupying the opening. She pauses, gestures impatiently
  for it to move aside, and waits while the bear slowly shifts enough for her
  to pass.
- **Endpoint:** The woman walks through; the bear remains where it was.

## Concept 7 — Picnic Cooler

- **Filming device:** Smartphone held by the older woman inside a camper
- **Framing:** Interior doorway view looking toward the picnic table; the
  camera does not leave the camper
- **Environment:** Quiet forest campground with an empty cooler beside a
  picnic table
- **Lighting:** Soft overcast morning light
- **Tone:** Nervous but restrained
- **Visual flow:** A bear drags the empty cooler a short distance, opens it,
  and investigates the empty interior. The older woman watches from inside
  the camper, then quietly closes the camper door without confronting it.
- **Endpoint:** The door closes while the bear remains focused on the empty
  cooler.

## Concept 8 — Empty Canoe

- **Filming device:** Fixed lakeside security camera
- **Framing:** Wide elevated view covering the dock, shoreline, and direction
  of the water current
- **Environment:** Rural cabin dock with one loosely tied canoe
- **Lighting:** Calm late-afternoon natural light
- **Tone:** Escalating visual absurdity
- **Visual flow:** A bear climbs into an unattended canoe. Its weight loosens
  the boat from the dock, and the canoe begins drifting with the current.
  Grandma notices and rushes toward the shore, but arrives after the canoe
  has already disappeared around the bend.
- **Endpoint:** Grandma stands at the empty shoreline; no extra chase or
  rescue action is added.

## Concept 9 — Sprinkler Play

- **Filming device:** Neighbor’s handheld smartphone
- **Framing:** Medium-wide observational view of the garden and sprinkler;
  the neighbor stays at a safe distance
- **Environment:** Ordinary suburban backyard flower garden
- **Lighting:** Bright summer afternoon
- **Tone:** Natural, quirky, observational
- **Visual flow:** The older woman is already tending the garden when a bear
  enters from off-frame. The sprinkler activates, and the bear begins
  playfully pawing at the water stream. The woman notices but does not
  approach or add another action.
- **Endpoint:** The bear continues playing in the spray; the clip ends on the
  visual behavior rather than adding a second human beat.

## Concept 10 — Porch-Step Sleeper

- **Filming device:** Doorbell camera
- **Framing:** Locked-off wide-angle view showing the full porch steps and
  walkway
- **Environment:** Small rural front porch with one clear, low bottom step
- **Lighting:** Soft early-morning light
- **Tone:** Dry, understated absurdity
- **Visual flow:** Grandma opens the door carrying a small package and sees a
  bear asleep across the bottom step. She pauses, makes one careful awkward
  hop over the bear, and continues down the walkway while the bear remains
  asleep.
- **Endpoint:** Grandma exits the frame; the bear does not react.

## Concept 11 — Garden-Hose Bear

- **Filming device:** Fixed backyard security camera
- **Framing:** Wide view of the hose spigot, lawn, and garden path
- **Environment:** Modest rural backyard during a hot day
- **Lighting:** Hard midday sunlight with ordinary security-camera exposure
- **Tone:** Matter-of-fact surprise
- **Visual flow:** A bear approaches the already-running garden hose and drinks
  directly from the stream. The older woman enters the existing garden path,
  sees the bear, and tells it to get out in a natural irritated voice. The
  bear keeps drinking for a moment before moving away.
- **Endpoint:** The bear leaves the hose; no repeated entrance or extra prop is
  added.

## Concept 12 — Trampoline Cubs

- **Filming device:** Fixed backyard night-security camera
- **Framing:** Wide elevated view covering the fenced yard, trampoline steps,
  and trampoline surface
- **Environment:** Ordinary suburban backyard at night
- **Lighting:** Grainy low-light footage; vehicle headlights briefly appear
  off-frame and then disappear
- **Tone:** Rare, joyful, plausible absurdity
- **Visual flow:** Three small bear cubs climb onto the trampoline and begin
  making small exploratory hops. Their bouncing becomes more enthusiastic.
  Headlights briefly wash across the yard, implying Grandma has arrived home.
  She enters through the existing gate, climbs the trampoline steps, and
  starts jumping with the cubs.
- **Endpoint:** Grandma and the cubs continue bouncing together in the fixed
  security-camera frame.

## Generation Gate

Before generating any of these concepts, confirm that the chosen version still
works as a believable captured moment and that the action is simple enough for
the selected video model. Create the storyboard only after the concept passes
that review. The storyboard should clarify continuity and action; it must not
turn the concept into a literal multi-panel composition in the generated
video.
