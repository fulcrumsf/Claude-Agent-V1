# Grandma And Bear Compilation

## Brief

This compilation presents distinct believable recordings in which ordinary
people treat a dangerous bear encounter as a familiar domestic or social
problem. The humor comes from the credible camera context and the human's
sincere, disproportionately casual attitude, not from adding a forced punch
line to every clip.

Reference 1 anchors the surveillance realism, while the other case studies
inform pacing, reversals, dialogue roles, and sound punctuation. Each clip must
have one clear event with a visible progression and natural endpoint. The long
form remains a standalone 16:9 compilation; narration will be written only
after the approved clips are assembled.

## Review Batch

1. **The dry-food warning** (estimated 8–12s; fixed body-camera perspective,
   officer approaches a porch; the bear is beside Grandma): The officer warns
   Grandma that the animal is wild, and she calmly explains that it refuses dry
   food. The joke is her sincere pet-owner attitude toward a wild bear; the
   officer's warning is setup, not a separate punchline. Optional in-scene
   dialogue: off-camera officer, then on-camera Grandma. No narrator needed.

2. **The swamp interruption** (estimated 10–14s; fixed rural security camera,
   elevated wide angle): Grandma sweeps near a porch while an alligator slowly
   approaches from the waterline. A bear already near the porch notices first,
   moves between them, and drives the alligator back toward the swamp. Grandma
   remains unaware until the danger has passed, then looks up at the bear as if
   it has interrupted her chore. No dialogue required; the action must remain
   physically staged and geographically plausible.

3. **The winter passenger** (estimated 8–12s; passenger's handheld phone aimed
   through a bus window, visible bus interior and reflections): In a cold,
   snowy town, the passenger notices an older woman driving a convertible with
   a large bear sitting in the passenger seat. The filmer steadies the phone and
   quietly reacts while the car passes; the bear remains inside the vehicle.
   The humor is the believable found-footage discovery, not an added joke.
   Off-camera passenger voice is optional and brief.

4. **The porch regular** (estimated 7–11s; fixed dusk doorbell camera, medium
   wide): A bear waits at the edge of the porch while Grandma opens the door
   with a small plate already in hand. She gives it one portion, closes the door,
   and the bear waits in the same place for the next portion. The behavior is a
   single readable feeding routine; do not add a bowl-return, hidden plate, or
   unexplained extra transaction.

5. **The driver's-seat mistake** (estimated 10–16s; fixed high-mounted gas
   station security camera, 16:9 wide-angle lens with mild fisheye distortion):
   Grandma opens the driver's-side door and finds the bear sitting in the
   driver's seat with the seat belt visibly retracted at the shoulder anchor.
   She pulls the same belt from that anchor across the bear's chest and lap,
   inserts the latch plate into the fixed receiver beside the bear's hip, then
   closes the door and walks away. This is intentionally a complex physics shot
   and must use a 16:9 storyboard with no more than six ordered frames; native
   video-model audio only, no Suno foley.
