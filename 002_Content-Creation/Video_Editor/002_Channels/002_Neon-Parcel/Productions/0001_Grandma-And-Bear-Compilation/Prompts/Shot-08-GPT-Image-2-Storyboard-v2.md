# Shot 08 Storyboard v2: Empty Canoe, Simplified Geometry

Create a clean 16:9 landscape storyboard reference for one continuous fixed outdoor security-camera shot. Use a four-panel 2-by-2 layout with a white caption band beneath each panel. Every panel must show the same small lakeside cabin, the same visible side door, the same short unobstructed grass-and-dirt path from the door to the shoreline, the same small dock, the same single red canoe, and the same lake bend.

Overall summary: A black bear climbs into a loosely tied canoe at a quiet cabin dock. The canoe drifts away with the current. Grandma exits the clearly visible cabin side door and runs down the open shoreline path, arriving too late. The route from the door to the shore must be physically continuous and obvious.

Tone and visual style: unplanned real-life outdoor security-camera footage, ordinary and believable, natural late-afternoon light, mild fixed-camera fisheye distortion, imperfect consumer/security-camera image quality, not cinematic, polished, staged, or commercial.

Spatial geometry lock: The cabin side door is visible on the left side of every panel. The door opens directly onto a short level ground path. The path runs continuously beside the cabin to the shoreline and has no deck railing, fence, wall, stairs, hidden corner, or obstacle between the door and the water. The dock is on the right side of the path. The canoe begins tied beside the dock and drifts straight toward the visible lake bend. Do not invent a wraparound railing or a second route.

FRAME 1: Wide establishing view. The cabin side door is closed and clearly visible on the left. The unobstructed ground path leads from the door to the dock and shoreline on the right. One red canoe is loosely tied beside the empty dock. No bear and no Grandma are visible. (Caption: 1. Empty dock. Canoe is loosely tied.)

FRAME 2: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. One large black bear is climbing from the shoreline into the single red canoe beside the dock. The cabin side door and continuous open path remain visible. Grandma is not yet visible. (Caption: 2. Bear climbs into the canoe.)

FRAME 3: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. The bear is seated inside the canoe as the canoe has pulled free and is drifting away from the dock toward the visible lake bend. The dock is now empty. The cabin side door remains visible and the path is still unobstructed. Grandma is not yet visible. (Caption: 3. Canoe comes loose and drifts away.)

FRAME 4: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. The canoe is farther away near the bend. Grandma has just exited through the clearly visible cabin side door and is running along the continuous open ground path toward the empty dock. She is on the path, never behind or through a railing. (Caption: 4. Grandma runs from the cabin, but the canoe is already gone.)

Final consistency check: exactly one cabin, one visible side door, one unobstructed ground path, one dock, one canoe, one bear, and one Grandma across the sequence. Preserve the fixed camera, shoreline, lake direction, dock position, cabin position, and path geometry. No wraparound railing, no hidden doorway, no impossible shortcut, no duplicate people or animals, no extra canoe, no camera movement, no alternate angle, no text inside image areas, and no commercial cinematic styling.
