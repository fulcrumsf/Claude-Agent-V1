# Shot 08 Storyboard v3: Bear Approaches Canoe, Grandma Chases Too Late

Create a clean 16:9 landscape storyboard reference for one continuous fixed outdoor security-camera shot. Use five chronological landscape panels in a single readable storyboard sheet, each with a white caption band beneath the image. Every panel must preserve the exact same simple lakeside cabin, open cabin side door, unobstructed ground path, shoreline rocks, short dock, single red canoe, lake, and downstream direction.

TONE: Unplanned real-life security-camera footage, ordinary, observational, imperfect, and believable. Natural late-afternoon light and mild fixed-camera fisheye distortion. Never cinematic, staged, polished, promotional, or commercial.

SPATIAL GEOMETRY LOCK: The cabin is on the left. The cabin side door opens directly onto a short, level, unobstructed grass-and-dirt path. The path runs continuously beside the cabin to the shoreline and dock. There is no deck railing, wraparound railing, fence, hidden corner, wall, stairs, or obstacle between the door and dock. Shoreline rocks are beside the water near the canoe. The dock is on the right. The canoe moves only downstream toward the visible bend. Grandma uses only the visible path and never passes through any railing or object.

FRAME 1: Wide fixed establishing view. The cabin side door is closed. One large black bear is already on the ground near the shoreline rocks beside the loosely tied red canoe, not inside it. Grandma is not visible. The open path from the cabin door to the dock is clearly readable. (Caption: 1. Bear waits by the shoreline rocks beside the canoe.)

FRAME 2: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. The same bear has walked a short distance from the rocks and is now on the shoreline between the rocks and the canoe, directly approaching the canoe. The canoe remains beside the dock and has not moved. Grandma is not visible. (Caption: 2. Bear approaches the canoe from the rocks.)

FRAME 3: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. The cabin side door is now open, and the same Grandma is peeking her head and shoulders through the doorway, just beginning to come out. At the shoreline, the same bear is climbing into the single red canoe. The canoe is still close to the dock. (Caption: 3. Grandma sees the bear as it climbs into the canoe.)

FRAME 4: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. Grandma has fully exited the visible cabin door and is running along the unobstructed ground path toward the dock. The bear is seated inside the canoe, which has come loose and is drifting a short distance downstream, still relatively near the dock. (Caption: 4. Grandma runs to the dock as the canoe starts drifting away.)

FRAME 5: Same fixed viewpoint and unchanged geometry. The bear remains seated in the same canoe, now farther downstream toward the bend. Grandma has reached the edge of the dock and stands there watching, too late to catch it. The dock, shoreline, path, cabin door, and water direction remain physically consistent. (Caption: 5. Grandma reaches the dock, but the bear is already farther away.)

FINAL CHECK: Show exactly one bear, one Grandma, one canoe, one dock, one cabin, and one open ground path. The bear's visible route is shoreline rocks to canoe to downstream water. Grandma's visible route is cabin doorway to ground path to dock edge. No subject appears from nowhere, no teleportation, no duplicate subjects, no canoe duplication, no impossible railing geometry, no walking through objects, no hidden shortcut, no camera movement, no alternate angle, no text inside image areas, and no commercial styling.
