## Reusable Humor and Retention Patterns
1.  **The Abrupt Reversal**: Establish an expectation (e.g., an animal is injured, an animal is being gentle) and then abruptly and completely subvert it with a sudden action (the animal is fine, the animal headbutts forcefully). This creates humor through surprise.
2.  **Anthropomorphic Pareidolia**: Frame an animal's action or sound in a way that suggests human-like intelligence, emotion, or speech (e.g., "he's saying 'mama'," "he looks like he has brain freeze"). Using on-screen text can prime the audience to perceive the human-like quality.
3.  **Escalation to Chaos**: Show a subject's simple desire (for food, for catnip) escalate in seconds to an uncontrolled, chaotic, and messy conclusion. The speed of the escalation is key to the comedic impact.
4.  **The Unconventional Tool**: Present a common problem and show it being solved by using an animal or object in a completely unexpected but surprisingly effective way (e.g., a cat as a bug-catcher). The humor lies in the ingenuity and absurdity of the solution.
5.  **Pacing Interrupt with Music**: In a rapid-fire montage of high-energy clips, insert a single, slow-paced, visually simple moment and score it with quirky, mood-setting music. This break in rhythm resets the viewer's expectations and makes the return to high-energy clips more impactful.
6.  **Setup-Punchline Juxtaposition**: Rapidly cut between short clips that represent different comedic formulas (e.g., a physical fail followed by a display of unexpected skill), creating a varied viewing experience that prevents predictability and maintains high engagement.
