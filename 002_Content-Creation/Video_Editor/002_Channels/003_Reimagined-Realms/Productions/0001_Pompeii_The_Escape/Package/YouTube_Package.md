## Description

18,000 people lived in Pompeii when Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD. Archaeologists have only ever found the remains of roughly 2,000. No one has explained where the other 16,000 went.

This is the story of the people history forgot — not the ones who stayed, but the ones who read the mountain correctly and walked away from everything they had ever owned.

The eruption had two phases. The first gave hours of warning. The second left no time at all. The survivors arrived in Rome, Naples, and Capua with nothing — no land, no property, no record. Roman society had no word for what they were. And not one of them ever wrote down what they saw.

The dead made Pompeii famous. The living disappeared completely.
