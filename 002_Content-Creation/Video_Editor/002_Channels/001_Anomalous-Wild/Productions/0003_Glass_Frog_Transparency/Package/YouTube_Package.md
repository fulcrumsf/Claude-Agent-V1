# Description

Glass frogs don't just have see-through skin — they can actively increase their own transparency on command. While sleeping, they pull nearly 90% of their circulating red blood cells out of general circulation and pack them into a mirror-coated liver, becoming 2-3 times more transparent than when awake.

In 2022, researchers used photoacoustic imaging to catch this happening in real time — and found something that should be medically impossible: concentrating that much blood this densely would normally cause fatal clotting. Glass frogs do it every single day without issue.

This video breaks down the transparent anatomy, the mirror-liver mechanism, and why cardiovascular researchers are now studying it for human medicine.
