# 2026-08-26 Feedback

## Neon Parcel Grandma/Bear Ideation

- Animal, environment, climate, and cultural setting must be believable as a
  complete combination before a concept is developed.
- Do not place a generic bear in an environment where that species would not
  naturally belong, such as a desert, just because the image could be made to
  look realistic.
- Choose the species from the location and ecology first. A Chinese rural
  setting may support a panda concept; a cold Russian setting may support a
  polar, black, or brown bear concept.
- Cultural and environmental specificity should create plausibility, not be
  decorative flavor added after the animal concept is chosen.
- Variation-scale clarification: Geographic and climate examples demonstrate
  the possible range, not a requirement to change countries or environments in
  every clip. The compilation subject controls the variation level. A normal
  compilation should feel like a curated set of believable submitted home
  videos, with mostly consistent regional plausibility and occasional subtle
  variation. A worldwide geography shift belongs only in a compilation whose
  stated subject is worldwide variation.

## Seedance 1.5 Extend Test: Bear Seat-Belt Physics

- The WaveSpeed Extend approach passed the believability bar despite minor
  hiccups and handled continuity better than the Kie split-image approach.
- Around 5 seconds, the model treated the belt as if it were already buckled
  and pulled upward on the shoulder portion. In a real car, an unbuckled belt
  should remain retracted at the shoulder anchor until it is pulled across the
  passenger.
- Around 10 seconds, the metal buckle tongue was adjusted but landed on the
  bear's chest instead of traveling toward the fixed buckle receptacle. The
  model needs stronger physical staging for the belt anchor, belt path, tongue,
  and receptacle.
- Around 15 seconds, Grandma walked away; this is a plausible natural endpoint
  for the first extension segment. She then returned, made a believable
  adjustment near the bear's head, and the door closed around 23 seconds.
- The truck door window was visible in the starting frame but disappeared when
  the door opened. Door and window geometry must be explicitly preserved across
  an extension; the model should not invent a windowless door.
- Approved test lesson: Seedance Extend is currently the preferred continuity
  experiment for multi-step actions, but prompts and/or start/end visual
  anchors still need explicit real-world object-physics constraints.

## Neon Parcel Shot Complexity Router

- Tony approved adding a pre-generation shot-complexity router to the Neon
  Parcel compilation pipeline.
- Simple shots should use Seedance 1.5 with start/end frames; physics-heavy
  shots should use Seedance 2 Mini with storyboard guidance and the approved
  upscale path; borderline shots must pause for human routing review.
- The router must be explainable, preserve human overrides, and never call a
  provider or spend credits by itself.
- Mini routing must generate at 480p, upscale 2x with Topaz, then normalize to
  final 1920x1080 with FFmpeg. Seedance 1.5 remains the native 1080p route.
- Complex-shot prompting must explicitly lock the capture source, camera
  height/angle/lens geometry, fixed objects, ordered visual states, and object
  path. Storyboards are checkpoints, not loose visual inspiration; the full
  output must be reviewed for drift and skipped states.

## Cross-Iteration Learning Expectation

- Tony expects repeated positive and corrective feedback to be generalized
  proactively into reusable skills, contracts, prompts, checks, or routing
  logic.
- Do not leave detailed lessons isolated in a case study or reduce them to
  generic guidance. Connect iteration results to the governing reusable
  artifact without waiting for an additional reminder.
- The seat-belt test established precise camera-lock, object-physics,
  ordered-state, and continuity rules. Those rules should have been carried
  into complex-shot prompting when the router was added.
