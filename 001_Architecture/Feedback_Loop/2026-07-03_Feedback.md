# Feedback Log — 2026-07-03

## Entry 1: Broad Audio Stems Are Wrong for Per-Scene Accuracy

**Feedback:** Tony rated the first audio pass 65% quality. The panicked_crowd stem was too loud and ran through a tonally mismatched scene (calm family of four walking at 0:37). The broader issue is that all 13 stems span multiple scenes and don't respond to what's actually visible on screen.

**Rule:** Never design audio stems that cover multiple visually distinct scenes. Each scene should have its own audio decision based on what is visually happening, not a broad thematic description derived from the beatmap.

**Why:** The beatmap describes story intent, not visual content. A stem timed to "act 2 chaos" will cover scenes where the chaos has already passed. Only visual analysis (Gemini video analysis or 1fps screenshots) can determine when a sound should actually start and stop.

**How to apply:** Before generating any audio for a future Reimagined Realms video (or any documentary-style video), run Gemini scene analysis on the stitched video first. Build the audio brief from visual content, not from beatmap descriptions.

## Entry 2: Audio Clips Are Not "Stems" — They Are Individually Generated Per-Scene SFX

**Feedback:** Tony clarified that the target architecture is NOT a stem map with broad looping sounds. Each audio clip should be generated for the specific visual content of that scene — a unique ElevenLabs SFX generation per scene — not a generic loop played through multiple scenes.

**Rule:** Per-scene audio clips = one unique ElevenLabs SFX prompt per scene, labeled by scene and timecode (`C05_0020_eruption_boom.mp3`). Do not reuse one generic clip across scenes even if the scenes feel similar.

**Why:** Generic looping sounds are detectable to the ear. A crowd panic loop that cycles mid-scene breaks immersion. A clip generated specifically for the visual content of that second sounds organic.

**How to apply:** `compose_audio.py` should output one clip per scene beat, with a unique prompt for each. The composer sees the screenshot, the Gemini text description, and the narration for each second.

## Entry 3: Impact Hits, Risers, and Transitions Are Required — Not Optional

**Feedback:** Tony noted the absence of impact sounds, tension-building risers, and transition whooshes. "The audio composer needs to take a class."

**Rule:** Every documentary-style video needs all three audio layers: (1) ambient beds, (2) spot FX event hits tied to specific visual moments, and (3) transition/riser sounds placed 2-3s BEFORE major cuts. Silence at a dramatic moment is a design choice, not a default.

**Why:** These are professional audio engineering standards. BBC Earth, Planet Earth II, and Our Planet use impact hits on every major eruption, animal strike, and reveal moment. Without them the video feels flat.

**How to apply:** The vision-based composer prompt must include documentary sound design knowledge covering risers, whooshes, sub-bass booms, and silence decisions. Research pass required before building the composer.

## Entry 4: Archive Before Overwrite — Always

**Feedback:** When re-running clip generation at corrected durations, Tony confirmed the right workflow is archive originals first, then overwrite.

**Rule:** Before running `--overwrite` on any batch of clips that need to change, move originals to `Video_Clips/Archive_<reason>/` folder first.

**Why:** Clips cost money to generate. If the new clips fail or come out wrong, there is no way to recover without re-spending. Archives are cheap storage.

**How to apply:** `batch_generate_videos.py` should check for existing files and ask for archive confirmation (or auto-archive to `Archive_<timestamp>/`) before overwriting.

## Entry 5: Validated — Seedance 1.5 at Exactly 12s Is Acceptable

**Tony accepted without pushback:** Using Seedance 1.5 Pro at exactly 12s for clips with a 12s final target. No padding buffer needed. This saves ~87% cost vs Seedance 2.0.

**Why it was non-obvious:** The original rule was "generate at target+1s padding". For exactly 12s clips, that would push to 13s and force Seedance 2.0. Tony confirmed the padding rule does not apply when target IS the model's maximum.

**How to apply:** Model selection rule is `Seedance 1.5 if generate_s ≤ 12, Seedance 2.0 if generate_s > 12`. When target_final_s = 12, generate at exactly 12s with 1.5. No ceiling-up.
