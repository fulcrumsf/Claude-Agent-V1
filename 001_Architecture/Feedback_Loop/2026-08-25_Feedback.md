## Neon Parcel Grandma/Bear Shot-List Draft

- Tony rejected the first generated shot-list concepts as poorly written,
  generic, and commercial-like. Specifically, Clips 1, 4, 5, and 6 failed.
- Do not generate another large 30-shot list before validating the humor
  direction with a small number of concepts.
- The pipeline must derive humor from the observed reference mechanics:
  believable found-footage context, a specific human motive or attitude,
  dangerous/extreme situation treated as ordinary, and a sharp character-based
  reversal or payoff.
- Avoid random household props, customer-service framing, or ad-like concepts
  unless the situation has a genuinely specific and believable comic reason.
- Every shot description must have an unambiguous subject for every action,
  explicit cause-and-effect, a motivated behavior, and a payoff that follows
  naturally. Avoid dangling pronouns or vague transitions such as "when it
  brings" without naming the actor and explaining the outcome.
- Dialogue must respond to visible or already-established context. Do not add
  lines such as "wait your turn" unless the shot visibly establishes a queue,
  competing animals, or another reason for that line. Hidden context used to
  force a punchline makes the scene incoherent.
- Do not add a second punchline after a clean visual joke has already landed.
  If the setup, action, and payoff are funny and complete, stop there instead
  of over-writing the scene with extra dialogue or explanation.
- Humor does not require an explicit verbal punchline. A believable recording
  of an absurd contradiction can be the complete humorous beat, especially a
  calm grandmother hand-feeding a wild bear while the camera treats it as an
  ordinary moment. Do not force extra joke-writing onto a naturally funny
  premise.
