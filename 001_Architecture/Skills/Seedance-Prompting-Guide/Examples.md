## A. Fixed doorbell camera — continuous wildlife encounter

**Inputs:** final labeled storyboard, full labeled environment sheet, courier character sheet, raccoon creature sheet, parcel prop sheet, in that order. Environment CAMERA POV must show a feasible walkway and porch. All storyboard panels share the doorbell vantage point and consistent subject scale.

**Purpose:** one continuous surveillance view, not a montage of storyboard panels.

```text
@Image 1 = approved storyboard; use its chronological action progression to guide one continuous scene. Its grid, labels, and borders are reference material, not the video layout.
@Image 2 = full labeled environment sheet titled "Porch"; its "CAMERA POV" panel defines the fixed doorbell view, and the other views clarify the same location.
@Image 3 = full labeled character sheet titled "Courier"; one courier throughout.
@Image 4 = full labeled creature sheet titled "Raccoon"; one raccoon throughout.
@Image 5 = full labeled prop sheet titled "Parcel"; one parcel throughout.

1. CAMERA LOCK
One uninterrupted fixed doorbell-camera recording at the CAMERA POV of @Image 2. Ordinary consumer-camera exposure and wide framing. Follow the action order in @Image 1. No camera pan, zoom, cut, or reframe.

2. SCENE CONTINUITY
Keep the porch, path, door, and railing of @Image 2 in consistent world positions. Keep @Image 3 human-sized relative to the door and @Image 4 raccoon-sized relative to the lowest step. The path to the porch remains unobstructed.

3. ACTION TIMELINE
[00:00-00:04] @Image 3 approaches along the visible path of @Image 2, carrying @Image 5 in both hands.
[00:04-00:07] @Image 3 sets @Image 5 beside the door and fully releases it. @Image 4 emerges from behind the lowest porch step.
[00:07-00:10] @Image 4 sniffs the corner of @Image 5 while @Image 3 notices and takes one startled step backward.
[00:10-00:14] @Image 4 stays beside @Image 5. @Image 3 retreats two steps down the same visible path, keeping clear of the railing.

4. AUDIO
Quiet outdoor ambience, footsteps on the path and porch, a soft cardboard contact sound, faint animal movement. No spoken dialogue or narration.

5. HARD CONSTRAINTS
One courier, one raccoon, one parcel. Preserve continuous motion and plausible size. Render the scene full-frame throughout.
- No music, no voiceover, no subtitles, no on-screen labels, no storyboard grid, no split screen, no borders, no duplicated subjects.
```

**Review:** verify stable mounting, no scale jump as the raccoon emerges, correct parcel release, and no grid or labels anywhere in the raw clip. This example does not claim anti-layout wording prevents leakage.

## B. Handheld phone — attention shifts and whip pans

**Inputs:** labeled storyboard, full labeled store environment sheet, bear creature sheet, shopper character sheet. The environment must establish doorway, aisle, shopper position, and the operator's retreat path. The storyboard must show these views as a feasible continuous phone recording.

**Purpose:** a person reacts while filming. The operator's changed position naturally changes the view; the store itself remains coherent. This illustrates Tony's requested style, not a mandatory bear scenario.

```text
@Image 1 = approved storyboard; its panels show successive moments of one handheld recording, including pans and the operator's retreat. Do not render its grid or labels.
@Image 2 = full labeled environment sheet titled "Corner Store"; "CAMERA POV" establishes the initial phone position. The plan and reverse views define the same connected space during camera movement.
@Image 3 = full labeled creature sheet titled "Bear"; one bear throughout.
@Image 4 = full labeled character sheet titled "Shopper"; one visible shopper throughout.

1. CAMERA LOCK
One continuous ordinary cellphone recording inside @Image 2, following @Image 1's action order. The operator holds the phone near chest height. Allow small hand tremors, imperfect framing, brief pan blur, and natural exposure adjustment. Whip pans are physical camera turns within the same recording, not cuts.

2. SCENE CONTINUITY
The entrance and checkout remain at their approved positions in @Image 2. @Image 3 fits through the doorway at the scale shown in the approved storyboard. @Image 4 remains beside the checkout. The operator has a clear aisle behind them for two short backward steps.

3. ACTION TIMELINE
[00:00-00:03] @Image 3 becomes visible at the entrance of @Image 2. An off-camera gasp draws the operator's attention; the phone whip-pans toward that entrance and settles on @Image 3.
[00:03-00:07] @Image 3 walks through the open doorway. The operator backs two short steps down the clear aisle, keeping @Image 3 roughly in frame with natural handheld bobbing.
[00:07-00:10] @Image 4 gives a startled nonverbal scream beside the checkout. The operator quickly pans toward @Image 4, briefly losing @Image 3 from view.
[00:10-00:15] @Image 3 continues along the same entrance aisle as the operator pans back and settles. @Image 3 has moved only the distance possible during the brief look away.

4. AUDIO
Store refrigerator hum, bear footfalls, the operator's shoes scuffing backward, an initial off-camera gasp, and @Image 4's nonverbal scream. Preserve the impression of sound recorded by the same phone.

5. HARD CONSTRAINTS
One continuous physical camera path. One bear and one visible shopper. Keep the aisle and doorway geometry coherent even while outside the frame.
- No cuts, no teleporting subjects, no cinematic coverage, no music, no voiceover, no spoken dialogue, no subtitles, no storyboard grid, no labels, no split screen.
```

**Review:** follow camera direction and operator position through both pans. Check that the bear does not teleport during the look away. Do not set a fixed-camera parameter or pin the CAMERA POV composition to every frame. If the sequence is rushed, simplify or extend/split using an approved route; do not assume five events must fit exactly.

## C. Handheld through a window — Seedance 1.5 Pro

**Inputs:** one clean opening scene image showing the room's window edge, yard, and a deer at plausible scale. Use sheets to build this image upstream; submit no sheets or contextual references to 1.5. No `@Image` tags.

**Purpose:** a stationary observer holding a phone, rather than a rigidly mounted surveillance camera.

```text
One continuous eight-second cellphone recording from inside the room, looking through the window shown in the starting image. The person filming stays behind the glass, holding the phone with small natural hand tremors.

The deer takes three slow steps across the yard, pauses beside the low fence, and turns its head toward a sound off to its right. The phone gently follows the deer without leaving the window opening. Keep the window edge and subtle glass reflections visible, with the yard remaining beyond the glass at its original depth. Preserve the deer's scale relative to the fence.

Faint indoor room tone and muted outdoor birds through the closed window. Ordinary phone exposure, slightly imperfect framing, no staged camera move.
- No dialogue, no narration, no music, no subtitles, no cuts, no camera passing through the glass, no duplicated deer.
```

**Review:** window remains a physical barrier; reflections should not become extra animals. Match the animal's initial pose and direction to the actual first image.

## D. Parked-car observer — Seedance 1.5 Pro first/last mode

**Inputs:** clean first frame from the passenger seat showing an animal approaching the side window; clean last frame from the **same camera viewpoint** showing it farther along the sidewalk. Keep door/window edges, lens perspective, lighting, and subject identity consistent. Submit through the actual temporal fields only.

**Purpose:** demonstrate meaningful subject progression without forcing a camera-angle change. Two images do not become two identity references.

```text
One continuous ten-second cellphone recording from the passenger seat of a parked car, looking out through the open side window. Keep the car stationary. The phone remains near the same position with small natural handheld movements.

The dog shown in the opening image walks along the sidewalk past the open window, pauses to look toward the person filming, then resumes walking to the position shown in the ending image. Preserve the same dog, sidewalk, and car interior. The door edge stays in the foreground; the phone does not leave the car.

Soft street ambience, distant traffic, the dog's footsteps, a brief collar jingle. Ordinary phone footage with natural exposure.
- No speech, no narration, no music, no subtitles, no cuts, no camera-angle jump, no extra dogs.
```

**Review:** end-frame consistency, continuous gait, coherent car edges, and a plausible pause. Similar camera composition is intentional and allowed; endpoints need not differ in angle.

## E. Multi-shot grid — storyboard as scene progression

**Model/mode:** Seedance 2.5 reference mode on a confirmed endpoint. **Camera:** deliberate multiple shots, unsuitable for a single CCTV take. **Evidence:** original adaptation of the pottery example in the [BytePlus-authored 2.5 guide](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5); no local test claimed.

Prepare a legible four-panel storyboard: workshop wide view → medium view of the maker shaping a bowl → close detail of the rim → finished bowl. Supply the maker and bowl references with explicit roles. The grid guides story and approximate framing, not an exact reproduction of every drawn detail. If used in a sheet-required pipeline, also include its approved environment/other sheets and remap all tags.

```text
@Image 1 = four-panel storyboard, read left to right across the top row, then left to right across the bottom row; reference its story progression and approximate shot composition.
@Image 2 = the maker's identity reference, the same person across all shots.
@Image 3 = the bowl design reference, used for the bowl being made and its final appearance.

Create a short workshop sequence in the order of @Image 1. Open wide with @Image 2 at the pottery wheel. Cut closer as @Image 2 shapes the clay. Cut to a detail of @Image 2 smoothing the rim of the bowl corresponding to @Image 3. Finish on the completed @Image 3 resting beside the wheel. Keep the workshop and lighting consistent across shots. Use quiet wheel noise, wet clay sounds, and room ambience. Render one full-frame shot at a time, with no storyboard layout, labels, captions, or music.
```

**Why this differs from A/B:** cuts are intended. The storyboard supplies coverage, not a promise of continuous camera motion. For a single take, redraw the board and rewrite its camera path rather than merely adding “no cuts” to contradictory coverage.

## F. Separate ordered keyframes — state and movement alignment

**Model/mode:** Seedance 2.5 contextual references. **Camera:** continuous fixed tabletop view. **Evidence:** original adaptation of the ordered-keyframe approach described in the [BytePlus-authored 2.5 guide](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5). These are not API first/last anchors.

Prepare four clean scene images from the same viewpoint: a toy car at the left end of a track → approaching the bridge → crossing the bridge → stopped at the right end. Verify wheel contact, car size, track geometry, and lighting before submission.

```text
@Image 1 = first reference state: the toy car at the left end of the tabletop track.
@Image 2 = second reference state: the same car approaching the bridge.
@Image 3 = third reference state: the same car crossing the bridge.
@Image 4 = fourth reference state: the same car stopped at the right end.

Use these four states in the listed order to guide one continuous fixed-camera view. The car rolls smoothly from left to right, approaches and crosses the bridge, then slows to a stop. Keep the same car, scale, track layout, light, and camera position throughout. Connect the states with continuous wheel movement and contact with the track. Quiet wheel rattle and room tone only. No cuts, grid, labels, subtitles, extra cars, or music.
```

**Review:** no reverse motion, size jumps, track changes, or duplicated car. Contextual state guidance can still drift. If hard opening/ending roles are required, use the endpoint's separate compatible temporal mode instead.

## Optional comparison tests — preserve the default

Run only when useful and approved. Never charge for an exploratory comparison merely because it appears here. Save prompt, model, endpoint, asset versions, settings, and results; keep all original sheets.

| Question | Baseline | Optional comparison | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Are multiple sheet views confusing identity? | Current full labeled character sheet | Clear headshot plus full-body derivative with explicit same-person roles | Duplicate people, identity, clothing, anatomy; do not change model simultaneously |
| Are labels leaking into output? | Current labeled sheets | Remove only nonessential text from a versioned derivative | Text leakage and whether spatial accuracy worsens |
| Is a grid causing layout copying? | Current reference storyboard | Smaller approved group of panels or separate ordered frames within limits | Layout leakage, omitted events, continuity |
| Does “every panel of @Image 1” help? | Approved prompt without treating that phrase as mandatory | A recorded wording-only comparison if worth testing | Camera consistency, grid copying; no outcome presumed |

Hold other variables constant where practical. If an endpoint supports a seed, record it, but do not promise exact reproducibility. Judge repeated comparable runs when feasible; one success cannot establish the cause. Promote useful results into a scoped local practice only after review, without pretending they are official requirements.
