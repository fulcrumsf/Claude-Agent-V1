# Low Poly Cyberpunk Thumbnail

## Purpose
Generate clean, high-CTR YouTube thumbnails in a distinctive **Low Poly Cyberpunk** style. This skill is designed for Claude Code, Codex, or any agent that writes thumbnail prompts for an image generator.

The goal is not to explain the entire video visually. The goal is to create a thumbnail that reads instantly on mobile, creates curiosity, and pairs with a strong title.

## Core Style Definition
A cinematic, low-poly, neon editorial thumbnail style with:

- Angular 3D characters, similar to video-game NPCs or Sims-like figures, but sharper and more faceted
- Cyberpunk lighting with teal, cyan, magenta, and neon lime accents
- Dark cinematic backgrounds with subtle paper grain and analog texture
- Origami-like geometric surfaces, as if people and objects are made from folded rice paper
- Minimal but dramatic composition
- One clear visual idea
- Little to no text
- Strong silhouette readability at small size

## Output Format
Default thumbnail format:

- Aspect ratio: **9:16 vertical**
- Intended platform: YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Reels
- Composition: mobile-first, readable at tiny size

If asked for long-form YouTube, use:

- Aspect ratio: **16:9 horizontal**

## Thumbnail Principles
Follow these rules strictly:

1. **One hero subject only**  
   Use one main character, product, object, or symbolic scene.

2. **One visual metaphor**  
   The image should communicate the story through contrast, not explanation.

3. **Minimal text**  
   Avoid title-like text unless explicitly requested. If text is needed, use 1–4 words maximum.

4. **No clutter**  
   Avoid adding multiple callouts, paragraphs, labels, diagrams, small objects, or dense backgrounds.

5. **Readable without text**  
   A viewer should understand the basic tension visually.

6. **Leave curiosity unresolved**  
   Do not give away the whole story in the thumbnail.

## Visual Language
Use these recurring style cues:

- Low-poly 3D characters with faceted angular skin and clothing
- Subtle paper-grain surface texture on faces, clothes, props, and background
- Neon rim lighting around the hero subject
- Dark teal or black background
- Cyan and magenta edge highlights
- Small neon symbols only when useful: arrows, X marks, glowing price tags, broken icons, warning marks
- Soft analog grunge overlays: dust, scratches, halftone, paper fibers
- Strong depth: foreground hero, simple symbolic background
- Cinematic contrast and glow

## Color Palette
Primary:

- Deep navy / black: background
- Cyan / teal: main highlight
- Magenta / pink: secondary rim light
- Neon lime / yellow: attention accent
- Warm beige / tan: paper texture

Use bright yellow sparingly unless the channel brand requires it.

## Character Design
Characters should look like:

- Angular low-poly 3D models
- Semi-realistic, not cartoonish
- Video-game cutscene style
- Clean, expressive posture
- No exaggerated facial expressions unless needed
- Slightly mysterious or dramatic

Avoid:

- Photorealistic portraits
- Cute cartoon characters
- Overly glossy plastic skin
- Horror-style faces
- Overly detailed likenesses of real people unless the user provides a clear public-figure instruction and the platform allows it

## Composition Template
Use this structure for most thumbnails:

```text
[Dark/simple background]
[One low-poly hero character or object taking 50–70% of frame]
[One symbolic object or visual clue]
[Neon rim lighting]
[Optional 1–4 word text block]
[Subtle paper texture over entire image]
```

## Prompt Builder
When given a topic, build the prompt using this formula:

```text
Create a 9:16 vertical YouTube Shorts thumbnail in a Low Poly Cyberpunk style.
The scene should visually communicate: [core idea].
Use one clear hero subject: [hero subject].
Add one simple symbolic clue: [symbolic clue].
Keep the composition clean, bold, and readable at mobile size.
Use angular low-poly 3D forms, neon teal and magenta rim lighting, dark cinematic background, subtle analog paper grain, and origami-like rice-paper texture on characters and objects.
Avoid clutter, avoid small text, avoid too many props, and do not explain the whole story.
Optional text: [1–4 words max, or none].
```

## Example: Warby Parker
```text
Create a 9:16 vertical YouTube Shorts thumbnail in a Low Poly Cyberpunk style.
Show a broke grad student in profile, angular low-poly 3D style, wearing glasses and holding a backpack strap.
Behind him, show a torn paper receipt reading "$700" with a red X across it, plus a faint Southeast Asia travel-map silhouette.
The image should suggest expensive glasses and frustration without explaining the whole story.
Use dark teal shadows, cyan rim light, subtle magenta edge glow, paper-grain texture, and origami-like geometric surfaces.
Keep it simple, premium, and readable at mobile size.
No headline text.
```

## Example: Apple Flops
```text
Create a 9:16 vertical YouTube Shorts thumbnail in a Low Poly Cyberpunk style.
Show one glowing Apple logo cracked down the middle, surrounded by only two simplified retro tech silhouettes.
Use dark cinematic background, neon cyan and magenta rim light, subtle yellow glow, low-poly geometric surfaces, and analog paper grain.
Make it visually read as failure or product flop without adding explanatory text.
Optional text: "APPLE FLOPS".
```

## Example: Business Origin Story
```text
Create a 9:16 vertical YouTube Shorts thumbnail in a Low Poly Cyberpunk style.
Show one low-poly founder-like character staring at a tiny failed product on a desk while a glowing future company icon appears faintly behind him.
Use one symbolic contrast: tiny problem versus massive future.
Keep it clean, dark, neon-lit, and readable.
Use subtle rice-paper grain and faceted origami-like geometry.
No extra text unless absolutely necessary.
```

## Negative Prompt / Avoid List
Avoid:

- Busy infographic layouts
- More than one main character
- Long text blocks
- Multiple labels or callouts
- Excessive arrows
- Tiny UI details
- Overly realistic photography
- Flat vector-only design
- Generic cyberpunk city clutter
- Brand names repeated everywhere
- Too many colors
- Explaining the entire story in the image

## Quality Checklist
Before finalizing a thumbnail prompt, verify:

- Can the thumbnail be understood in less than 1 second?
- Is there only one main visual idea?
- Would it still work if the viewer did not read any text?
- Is the title doing the explaining instead of the thumbnail?
- Does the image create curiosity instead of answering everything?
- Is the composition uncluttered at phone size?

## Best Use Cases
This style works well for:

- Business origin stories
- Product failures
- Weird startup pivots
- Tech history
- Founder stories
- Explainer videos
- Strategy videos
- Mystery-driven educational shorts
