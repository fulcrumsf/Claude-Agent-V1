# Skill: Mythology & Folklore Research

Use this skill for any research task involving mythology, folklore, ancient mysteries, historical anomalies, collective legends, lost histories, or strange coincidences across cultures.

---

## When to Use

- User asks for a story idea for **Glyphary**, **Polyoculis**, **Reimagined Realms**, or **Kingdoms & Conquerors**
- User asks to research a specific myth, legend, or folklore topic
- User wants to find culturally parallel myths across civilizations
- User wants the "strange truth" behind a historical event or ancient mystery
- User wants a hook-ready story about a lost city, forgotten place, or documented anomaly

---

## Research Protocol

### Step 1: Clarify the Channel + Direction

Before researching, confirm:
1. **Which channel?** (Glyphary, Polyoculis, Reimagined Realms, Kingdoms & Conquerors, or open)
2. **Specific topic or broad?** (e.g., "Norse mythology" vs. "find something surprising in any tradition")
3. **Short or long-form?** (determines how deep the story structure needs to go)

If channel is unspecified, default to Glyphary tone.

---

### Step 2: Research Stack (use all relevant tools)

Run research across multiple layers:

#### Layer 1 — Primary Sources (use WebSearch + WebFetch)
- Wikipedia for overview + cross-references
- Ancient texts repositories: sacred-texts.com, theoi.com (Greek), voluspa.org (Norse)
- JSTOR or academic PDFs for obscure folklore
- Search terms: `"[myth name] etymology"`, `"[culture] flood myth"`, `"[artifact] archaeological controversy"`

#### Layer 2 — The Unusual Angle (always look for this)
The hook is never "here is a myth." The hook is the **thing about the myth that nobody expected**:
- A version that contradicts the popular retelling
- Evidence that the myth was literal — a real event behind it
- The same myth appearing in 4 unconnected cultures
- The myth that was actively suppressed or mistranslated
- A physical object or place that shouldn't exist if the myth is fiction

Search specifically for:
- `"[myth] real evidence"`
- `"[myth] archaeological proof"`
- `"[culture] myth [parallel culture] same story"`
- `"[topic] dismissed by mainstream archaeology"`

#### Layer 3 — Cultural Cross-Reference
For every strong myth or legend, check if it appears across cultures:
- The flood narrative (appears in 200+ cultures — but which version is strangest?)
- The underworld / land of the dead (Greek Hades, Norse Helheim, Mayan Xibalba, Japanese Yomi)
- The world-tree / axis mundi (Norse Yggdrasil, Hindu Ashvattha, Mayan Wacah Chan)
- Giants, serpents, tricksters, abandoned children who become gods

Cross-cultural parallel = Glyphary gold. The fact that separated civilizations encoded the same story is the mystery.

---

### Step 3: Story Structure (by channel)

#### For Glyphary
Structure the story as **evidence presented, never explained**:
```
Opening: The strangest image or quietest fact first.
  → Don't say "In ancient Greece..." Say: "Three thousand years ago,
    a city burned. Every account agrees on what started the fire.
    None of them agree on what started it."

Body: Present evidence in layers — each one makes the mystery
    stranger, not simpler. Never resolve. Add, complicate, deepen.

Close: End on the question, not the answer. The wonder is in
    the not-knowing.
```

**Glyphary story angles that hit:**
- The myth archaeologists proved true (Troy, Sodom, the Flood)
- The thing passed down orally for 10,000 years before writing existed
- Two cultures that never met — same story, same detail
- The document/artifact that was "lost" but still quoted by everyone who read it
- The map, the codex, the calendar that doesn't match what we think we know

#### For Polyoculis
Structure as **explainer with a counterintuitive anchor**:
```
Opening: The most surprising true fact. Specific. Precise.
  → "The Nazca Lines are 2,000 years old. They can only be seen
    from the air. The Nazca people had no aircraft."

Body: Vox-quality explanation — what we know, what we don't,
    the best current theory, and exactly where that theory breaks down.

Close: Why this matters / what remains genuinely unanswered.
    Signal that smart people still disagree.
```

**Polyoculis mythology angles that hit:**
- Ancient engineering that modern engineers struggle to replicate
- Geographic anomaly with a mythological explanation that turned out to be correct
- The culture that "predicted" an astronomical event centuries before the math existed
- Archaeological discoveries that mainstream media ignored

#### For Reimagined Realms
First-person POV from inside the historical moment:
```
"You are [role] in [civilization] on the day [event] changes everything.
Here is what you would have known. Here is what you could not have known.
Here is the decision that determined whether your name survived."
```

#### For Kingdoms & Conquerors
Frame the mythological element as **strategic or military leverage**:
- How a ruler used their people's mythology to justify conquest
- The prophecy that an empire built its entire expansion around
- The religious symbol weaponized as a banner
- The myth that two enemies shared — and what they each did with it

| [sacred-texts.com](https://www.sacred-texts.com) | World mythology archive — Hindu, Norse, Egyptian, Mayan, Native American |

| Google Scholar | Search: `"[myth] comparative" site:jstor.org` |
| Wikipedia "See Also" chains | Start with main article, follow cross-cultural links |

### For Archaeological Anomalies
| Search pattern | What it surfaces |
|---|---|
| `"[myth] confirmed archaeologically"` | Myths that turned out to be literal |
| `"[culture] pre-dates accepted timeline"` | Evidence challenging standard dating |
| `"[structure] purpose unknown"` | Genuine archaeological mysteries |
| `"[myth] suppressed" OR "revised" OR "mistranslated"` | Hidden story angles |

### For Cross-Cultural Parallels
Search: `"[theme] myth comparative religion site:academia.edu"`
The best parallels: flood myths, trickster gods, underworld journeys, world-tree cosmologies, sky-father/earth-mother splits, the hero who dies and returns.

## Quick Idea Generators

When user wants a cold start ("find me something for Glyphary"), use these angle prompts:

**The Impossible Artifact:**
"Find a physical object that exists today that should not exist if the mythology around it is fiction."
→ Examples: Antikythera mechanism, Baghdad Battery, Piri Reis map, Saqqara Bird

**The Suppressed Version:**
"Find a myth whose popular version erases a darker or stranger original."
→ Examples: Original Grimm fairy tales, pre-Christian European saints who were pagan gods, the Book of Enoch

**The Cross-Cultural Twin:**
"Find two myths from civilizations with no contact that tell the same story."
→ Examples: Great flood (Mesopotamian Utnapishtim + Genesis + Mayan + Hindu Manu), dying-and-rising god (Osiris, Dionysus, Persephone, Christ)

**The Oral Memory:**
"Find a myth that oral tradition preserved accurately for thousands of years before archaeology confirmed it."
→ Examples: Aboriginal Australian star maps 10,000+ years old, Dogon star knowledge, Scandinavian oral history of volcanic eruptions

**The Deliberately Forgotten:**
"Find a culture, story, or deity that was actively erased and what survived anyway."
→ Examples: Sappho's poetry, the Library of Alexandria's contents, the Cathars, pre-Christian Irish goddess traditions
