# AI Essentials: Foundational Knowledge

## What It Is
A structured primer on core AI concepts — covering the difference between AI, ML, Generative AI, LLMs, and Agentic AI, how LLMs are built, and the mindset required to use AI effectively without falling into common traps.

## Key Concepts

- **AI** — machines performing tasks requiring human intelligence (reasoning, learning, problem-solving)
- **Machine Learning** — systems that learn from data without explicit programming; powers fraud detection, recommendations, anomaly detection
- **Generative AI** — creates new content (text, images, code) from learned patterns
- **LLMs** — trained on vast text corpora via self-supervised next-word prediction, then instruction-tuned and behaviorally aligned
- **Agentic AI** — autonomous systems combining ML, Gen AI, and NLP to achieve complex goals without constant human oversight

## How LLMs Are Built (3 Stages)
1. **Training** — billions of words, learns grammar/facts/relationships via next-word prediction
2. **Tuning** — instruction tuning (follow directions) + behavioral alignment (tone, personality)
3. **Implementation** — connected to org knowledge bases, industry data, workflow context

## Key Value Drivers
- **Automation** — complex tasks once impossible to standardize
- **Personalization** — context-aware content at scale
- **Productivity** — teams focus on strategy, AI handles execution

## Limitations to Watch
- **Hallucinations** — AI confidently states false information; never trust facts/figures/citations without verification
- **Generic outputs** — poor prompting produces poor results
- **Data/privacy risks** — what you feed in matters

## Data-First vs AI-First Mindset
- **Data-first**: treat data as strategic asset, validate quality before building agents
- **AI-first**: ask if AI is the right tool, confirm dataset supports the use case
- Rule: build agents only after a strong data-first foundation

Reference for grounding conversations with clients, collaborators, or new team members who need AI context. Also useful when evaluating whether a new tool is genuinely ML-powered vs. marketing fluff.
