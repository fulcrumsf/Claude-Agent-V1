# Robotto Gato Channel Strategy

## What It Is
A future YouTube channel concept, not yet launched: a practical AI creator-tech channel that reviews AI software, teaches specific workflows (e.g. building a video pipeline), and documents the creator's real experience using these tools — "learn by building, explain what actually happened," not polished outcome reels or bare tool lists.

**Status: a plan to review, not an active production.** Full detail lives in the source plan; this page is a summary for quick orientation.

## Positioning
"For creators who want to use AI to make better content and build repeatable workflows, Robotto Gato offers honest software reviews and hands-on walkthroughs grounded in real projects." Working promise: "I test the tools and build the workflow in public, so you can see what works, what doesn't, and how to adapt it." Explicitly avoids easy/guaranteed-income claims — authority comes from showing the work and the tradeoffs.

## Three Content Pillars
1. **AI software, tested** — reviews, comparisons, pricing/value, limitations.
2. **Creator workflows** — step-by-step builds for a specific outcome (e.g. "idea to published Short").
3. **Build in public** — experiments, failures, revisions, lessons.

## Monetization Ladder (sequenced, not simultaneous)
Affiliate links (early) → clipping/UGC tech campaigns (after a portfolio exists) → Amazon Associates gear reviews → downloadable guides/worksheets (once a workflow is proven) → prompt/template packs → Skool community/course (later, only after repeated demand). Explicitly: do not build a course before the audience and demand are clear.

## First 90 Days (proposed pacing)
Weeks 1-2 foundation → weeks 3-6 first series → weeks 7-10 iterate from evidence (retention, comments) → weeks 11-13 package one proven workflow into a free checklist. One long-form video every 1-2 weeks, sustainable pace over frequent pivots.

## Open Decisions (per the plan itself, unanswered)
- Which first project anchors the launch series (a video pipeline build is the plan's own leading candidate).
- Realistic publishing cadence given actual available time.
- Which creator type the first 5-10 videos serve most directly.
- Channel voice/visual identity.
- Initial monthly software/gear budget.
